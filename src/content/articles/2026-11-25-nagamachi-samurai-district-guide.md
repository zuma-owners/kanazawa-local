**Short answer:** Nagamachi (長町) is a small area of earthen walls, stone lanes and canals where Kanazawa's samurai once lived, about 5 minutes on foot from the Korinbo bus stop. Walking the streets is **free** and has no opening hours. Allow **about an hour**, or closer to two if you go inside the houses. The one paid house most people visit is the **Nomura family residence** (¥550 for adults). Several other places are free, including a museum of foot soldiers' houses and a rest house with toilets and volunteer guides.

Nagamachi is easy to do badly: people walk one lane, take a photo of a wall and leave in ten minutes. The interesting parts are behind the gates, and it is not obvious from the street which gates you can go through. This guide sorts that out.

*Information checked on the Kanazawa City Tourism Association's Japanese site and the Nomura house's official site on November 25, 2026. Prices and hours change, so check before you go.*

## What is the Nagamachi samurai district?

In the Edo period, this was a neighbourhood for samurai of the Kaga domain. What survives is the street plan: narrow lanes, mud-coloured earthen walls (*dobei*) topped with tiles, wooden gates, and the Onosho canal running alongside. The tourism association calls Onosho the oldest canal in Kanazawa, and the city protects the area as a traditional landscape zone.

One thing to know before you go: this is **not a museum village**. The tourism association's Japanese guide says plainly that most of the houses are ordinary homes with people living in them, and that some visitors mistake the area for a theme park. So the rule is simple. If a gate has a sign and a ticket desk, go in. If it does not, it is somebody's house.

### The Nomura house

This is the main sight, and the only one where you see a full samurai-class house with its garden. Visitors walk through the rooms, including a formal reception room with painted sliding doors, and the Nomura family's swords and armour are on display. You look out at the garden from the veranda, and there is also a tea room. Plan about 30 minutes.

**A price change is coming.** The house has announced on its Japanese news page that from **April 1, 2027**, admission becomes ¥750 for adults (high school students included) and ¥350 for elementary and junior high school children. Until then, the prices in the table apply. The official site has no working English page at the moment, so this is easy to miss.

### The free places

- **Ashigaru Museum.** Two real houses of *ashigaru*, the foot soldiers at the bottom of the samurai ranks, moved here and opened to the public. The roofs are held down with stones. It takes about 20 minutes and makes a good contrast with the Nomura house: same city, same period, a very different life.
- **Takada family house remains.** A long gatehouse with stables and a servants' room that you can look into, plus a small garden with a pond. About 15 minutes.
- **Nagamachi rest house.** Toilets, a place to sit, and pamphlets. It is also where the volunteer guides wait (see below).

### Two small museums

The **Maeda Tosanokami Family Museum** shows documents and objects from the Maeda Tosanokami family, one of the domain's high-ranking samurai families, and the **Shinise Memorial Hall** nearby is an old pharmacy building with its shopfront recreated. Both are small, and both close on Mondays.

## Can I get a guide?

Yes, and it is free. Kanazawa's volunteer guides, called *Maido-san* (まいどさん), are stationed at the Nagamachi rest house. According to the tourism association's Japanese pages, small groups can ask on the spot without a reservation, and a walk takes roughly 30 to 60 minutes. Groups of ten or more should arrange it in advance.

The pages I checked do not say which languages are available, so do not count on an English tour. If you speak a little Japanese, or just want someone to point out what you would otherwise walk past, it is worth asking.

## What are the straw mats on the walls in winter?

From **early December to mid-March**, the earthen walls are covered with woven straw mats. This is *komo-gake* (薦掛け). The mats stop snow from sticking to the walls and stop the earth from cracking when it freezes. Like the [snow ropes on the pines in Kenrokuen](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/), it is practical work that has become one of the signs of winter in Kanazawa.

So if you are here in winter, Nagamachi looks different from the photos in most guidebooks, and many people think it looks better. The tourism association does not publish exact dates for when the mats go up, so I cannot promise them on a particular day in early December. For what else changes in the cold months, see my [Kanazawa winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/).

## How do I get there?

- **By bus:** get off at **Korinbo** (香林坊) and walk about 5 minutes. Both directions of the Kanazawa Loop Bus and many regular buses from the station stop there. My [Kanazawa bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) explains which bus to take and how to pay.
- **By community bus:** the small Kanazawa Flat Bus (Nagamachi route) has a stop called **Nagamachi Bukeyashiki Ato** (長町武家屋敷跡), right in the district.
- **On foot:** if you are staying downtown around Korinbo or Katamachi, just walk.
- **By car:** the lanes are narrow and there is no general car park in the district. The tourism association suggests the city-run Nagamachi tourist car park nearby.

## When should I go?

The streets never close, so early morning and late afternoon are the quiet times for photos. For the houses, remember that the Nomura house opens at 8:30, earlier than everything else here, and that from October to March it closes at 16:30.

Avoid planning the two small museums for a Monday. Around the New Year, check each place: the Nomura house closes on December 26–27 and January 1–2, and the two museums close for the New Year holidays.

If you want a coffee before or after, [MORON CAFE](/articles/moron-cafe-kanazawa/) is in Nagamachi and opens at 9:00.

## Before you go

- The streets are **free**. Allow about an hour, more if you go inside.
- **Nomura house:** ¥550 for adults now, ¥750 from April 1, 2027.
- **Free:** the Ashigaru Museum, the Takada house remains and the rest house.
- People live here. Only go through gates that are clearly open to visitors.
- Winter visitors: look for the straw mats on the walls from early December.
