**Short answer:** Kanazawa Castle Park (金沢城公園) is **free to enter** and open every day, 8:00 to 17:00 from mid-October to the end of February and 7:00 to 18:00 from March to mid-October. Most of what you see, including the Ishikawa Gate, two rebuilt gates and the Gyokusen-inmaru garden, costs nothing. The only paid part is the inside of the rebuilt towers and long storehouse (Hishi Yagura, Gojikken Nagaya and Hashizume-mon), which is **¥320 for adults**. Allow about an hour, or 90 minutes if you go inside.

There is no main keep to climb. No castle keep stands here today, so if you arrive expecting a tall white tower like Himeji, you will be confused. What you get instead is a huge park of stone walls, white gates and rebuilt wooden buildings, right next to Kenrokuen.

*Information checked on Ishikawa Prefecture's official Kanazawa Castle Park site (Japanese pages) on November 26, 2026. Hours and prices change, so check the [official site](https://shiro-niwa.pref.ishikawa.lg.jp/kanazawa-castle/) before you go.*

## Is Kanazawa Castle Park free?

Yes. The park grounds have no entrance fee and no closing days. The prefecture's site lists these hours:

| Season | Park hours |
|---|---|
| March 1 to October 15 | 7:00–18:00 |
| October 16 to end of February | 8:00–17:00 |
| Night openings (see below) | until 21:00 |

The park also opens its gates even earlier for walkers, from 6:00 in winter (November to February) and from 4:00 or 5:00 in the warmer months, though some facilities are not available then. If you are doing the [free early entry at Kenrokuen](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/), you can walk straight across to the castle afterwards.

## What is free to see?

- **Ishikawa Gate (石川門).** The gate facing Kenrokuen and the one in most photos. It was rebuilt in **1788** after fires and has survived since then. It is a national Important Cultural Property, and the official site points out that only Osaka Castle's Otemon and this gate still have their whole gate compound standing as it was in the Edo period.
- **Kahoku Gate (河北門).** Rebuilt in 2010. You can go inside for free (9:00–16:30, last entry 16:00).
- **Nezumita Gate and bridge (鼠多門・鼠多門橋).** A black-and-white gate with a 32.6-metre wooden bridge, both rebuilt in **2020**. Also free inside, same hours. This is the gate that connects the castle with Oyama Shrine and the Nagamachi side of town.
- **Gyokusen-inmaru Garden (玉泉院丸庭園).** A daimyo pond garden first laid out in 1634, lost at the end of the domain era and recreated in 2015. It sits low beside the castle walls, with a height difference of about 22 metres, so the stone walls become part of the view.
- **Tsurunomaru rest house (鶴の丸休憩館).** Free, with seats, exhibits and light food. It also shows a 10-minute VR video of the Ninomaru Palace, the castle's main residence, which burned down in 1881.

## What do you get for ¥320?

The paid area is one connected set of rebuilt buildings: **Hishi Yagura** (a diamond-shaped corner tower), **Gojikken Nagaya** (a long tower building joining them), **Hashizume-mon Tsuzuki Yagura** and **Hashizume-mon** gate. They were first built in 1632, burned in 1881 and were rebuilt in 2001 using traditional wooden construction. Inside, you walk through the timber structure and look out over the grounds.

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| Adults (18 and over) | ¥320 |
| Ages 6 to 17 | ¥100 |
| Under 6 | Free |
| Kenrokuen + one castle facility combined ticket | ¥500 |
| Visitors 65 and over (with ID) | Free |

Hours are **9:00–16:30**, last entry 16:00, open every day. The official fee page lists cash, cards and e-money as payment options.

If you are visiting both, the combined ticket with Kenrokuen saves a little. The over-65 exemption is worth knowing: bring your passport as ID.

Is it worth it? If you like wooden buildings and joinery, yes. If you only have an hour and the weather is good, the free gates and the garden give you most of the castle experience.

## When are the night openings?

According to the prefecture's night-opening page, the park stays open from **sunset until 21:00 on Saturdays** and during busy seasons such as cherry-blossom time. Entry is free, through the Ishikawa Gate, Nezumita Gate and Gyokusen-inmaru entrances. The Gyokusen-inmaru garden is lit up on those evenings, and the outer stone walls are lit every night until 22:00.

There are also seasonal light-up events shared with Kenrokuen, including a winter one. I have not found the official dates for winter 2027 yet, so see my [Kanazawa winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/) and check the official site closer to the time.

## Can I get a free guide?

Yes. Two volunteer groups guide visitors for free, with no booking needed:

- **Japanese-language guides:** 9:30–15:30, every day from March 1 to December 28, and weekends only from January 5 to the end of February. They wait at the Ishikawa Gate rest area, the Tsurunomaru rest house and the Gyokusen-an rest house.
- **Foreign-language guides:** mainly English, sometimes Chinese or Korean, 9:30–15:30, in principle every day except around the New Year.

The details are clearest on the Japanese page of the official site, so here they are in English.

## What is being built in the middle of the park?

The **Ninomaru Palace (二の丸御殿)**. In the Edo period it was the Kaga domain's residence and government office, with more than 60 rooms, the biggest building in the castle. The prefecture is rebuilding it. According to the official site, construction of the main building started in earnest with a ceremony in **April 2026**, and the first phase is aimed for completion **around 2033**. You will see work areas for years. It does not close the park, but expect some fenced-off spaces.

## Which entrance should I use?

The park has several gates, each with its own nearest bus stop (from the official access page):

| Entrance | Nearest bus stop | Walk |
|---|---|---|
| Ishikawa Gate (from Kenrokuen) | Kenrokuen-shita / Kanazawa Castle (兼六園下・金沢城) | 5 min |
| Gyokusen-inmaru (garden side) | Hirosaka / 21st Century Museum (広坂・21世紀美術館) | 5 min |
| Nezumita Gate (via Oyama Shrine) | Minamicho / Oyama Shrine (南町・尾山神社) | 5 min |
| Kuromon (toward the market) | Musashigatsuji / Omicho Market (武蔵ヶ辻・近江町市場) | 7 min |

A route that works: enter through the **Ishikawa Gate** after Kenrokuen, walk through to the paid towers, then leave through **Gyokusen-inmaru** for the [21st Century Museum](/articles/21st-century-museum-guide/), or through **Nezumita Gate** toward Oyama Shrine and the [Nagamachi samurai district](/articles/nagamachi-samurai-district-guide/). Coming from [Omicho Market](/articles/omicho-market-guide/), the Kuromon entrance is closest. My [bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) explains which buses stop where.

## Before you go

- The park is **free** and open every day. Hours change on March 1 and October 16.
- The towers cost **¥320** (9:00–16:30). Combined ticket with Kenrokuen: ¥500.
- No keep to climb. The highlights are the gates, the stone walls and the garden.
- **Saturday evenings:** free until 21:00, with the garden lit up.
- **Free volunteer guides**, including English, no booking needed.
