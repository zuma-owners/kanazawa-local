Most people give Kanazawa one day. They arrive on the Shinkansen in the morning, walk around Kenrokuen garden, take a photo in the old teahouse district, and leave in the evening for Kyoto or Tokyo.

You can do that, and it is a good day. But Kanazawa is a city that rewards an extra night. The main sights are close together, the food is a big part of the trip, and the evenings are a side of the city day-trippers never see.

I teach English just outside Kanazawa, and this is the plan I would give a friend visiting for the first time. Pick the version that matches the time you have.

## Getting here

**From Tokyo**, the Hokuriku Shinkansen runs straight to Kanazawa Station. The fastest trains, called *Kagayaki*, take about two and a half hours. Slower trains on the same line take around three hours.

**From Kyoto or Osaka**, the Shinkansen line now ends at Tsuruga, south of Kanazawa. You take the *Thunderbird* limited express to Tsuruga and change to the Shinkansen there. Check the current timetable on a route app before you travel. My [guide to getting to Kanazawa](/articles/getting-to-kanazawa/) explains the change at Tsuruga step by step.

Kanazawa Station itself is worth a minute. The big wooden gate in front of the East Exit is one of the most photographed things in the city, and you will walk right through it.

## Before you plan: three timing rules

These are the things that trip up first-time visitors most often.

1. **Kenrokuen opens early, and early is free.** The garden's regular hours are 7:00 to 18:00 from March 1 to October 15, and 8:00 to 17:00 from October 16 to the end of February. Adult admission is ¥320. Before the regular hours, some gates open for free early entry. The start time changes with the season (as early as 4:00 in summer, 6:00 in winter), and you have to leave before paid hours begin. Check the official Kenrokuen page for the current gates, or see my [Kenrokuen guide](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/) for the full table.
2. **The 21st Century Museum is closed on Mondays.** More exactly, the ticketed exhibitions are. The free areas of the building stay open from 9:00 to 22:00 every day. The exhibitions run 10:00 to 18:00, until 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays, and are closed on Mondays (or the next day when Monday is a holiday). The museum has also announced a long closure for renovation, from May 2027 to March 2028, so check before you go if you are visiting then. My [21st Century Museum guide](/articles/21st-century-museum-guide/) has the details on tickets, free areas and The Swimming Pool.
3. **The "Ninja Temple" needs a reservation.** Myoryuji, the temple famous for its hidden traps and staircases, can only be seen on a guided tour, and you need to book by phone in advance. If you turn up without a booking, you will probably be turned away. My [Ninja Temple guide](/articles/ninja-temple-myoryuji-guide/) explains how to book.

## If you have one day

**Morning: Kenrokuen and Kanazawa Castle Park.** Start at the garden as early as you can. It is far calmer in the first hours. [Kanazawa Castle Park](/articles/kanazawa-castle-park-guide/) is right next to it, across a bridge, so you can walk from one to the other.

**Late morning: the 21st Century Museum.** It is a short walk from Kenrokuen. If it is not a Monday, see an exhibition. If it is, you can still walk through the free areas of the building.

**Lunch: Omicho Market.** Kanazawa's main fresh market is a short bus ride from the museum area. It is the place to eat seafood at lunchtime, and in winter it is full of crab. See my guide to [crab season in Kanazawa](/articles/kanazawa-crab-season/) for what to look for, and my [Omicho Market guide](/articles/omicho-market-guide/) for hours, lockers and the best time to go.

**Afternoon: Higashi Chaya district.** The old teahouse street, with its wooden lattice fronts, is the image most people have of Kanazawa. It gets busy in the middle of the day. Late afternoon is often more pleasant. My [Higashi Chaya guide](/articles/higashi-chaya-district-guide/) covers the two teahouses you can go inside.

**Evening:** If you are leaving, Kanazawa Station has places to eat before your train. If you can stay, read on.

## If you have two days

Do the one-day plan, but split it so nothing is rushed, and add an evening.

**Day 1**
- Arrive, drop your bags, and go to **Omicho Market** for lunch.
- Afternoon in **Higashi Chaya**, then walk along the Asano River nearby.
- Evening: dinner and drinks downtown. Most of Kanazawa's nightlife is in **Katamachi** and the small street next to it, **Kiguramachi**. My [guide to Kanazawa's nightlife districts](/articles/kanazawa-nightlife-areas/) explains how to get there and what each area feels like.

**Day 2**
- Early morning at **Kenrokuen**, then **Kanazawa Castle Park**.
- Late morning at the **21st Century Museum**.
- Afternoon: the [samurai district (Nagamachi)](/articles/nagamachi-samurai-district-guide/) or the temple district across the Sai River, where Myoryuji is, if you booked a tour.

If ordering dinner in Japanese worries you, read [how to order at an izakaya](/articles/izakaya-english-kanazawa/) before your first night. Ten short phrases cover almost everything.

## If you have three days

Use the two-day plan, then make day three a day trip.

**Shirakawa-go** is the most popular choice. The village of steep-roofed farmhouses is about 1 hour 25 minutes from Kanazawa by express bus. Two things to know:

- **Book the bus early** in busy seasons, especially in winter, when the snowy village is at its most famous.
- **Luggage is a problem.** There is very little space to store bags in the village. If you are moving on after Shirakawa-go, plan where your suitcase will be.
- My [Shirakawa-go day trip guide](/articles/shirakawa-go-day-trip/) covers the bus, booking and luggage in detail.

If you prefer to stay in the city, a slower third day works well too: go back to the places you liked, spend more time in the museum, or simply have a long lunch and a second night out.

## Where to stay

For a short trip, there are two sensible choices:

- **Near Kanazawa Station**, if you are arriving and leaving by train and want the easiest transfers.
- **Downtown, around Korinbo and Katamachi**, if you want to walk to dinner and be close to the nightlife.

Buses and taxis connect the two, so neither choice is wrong. My [where-to-stay guide](/articles/where-to-stay-kanazawa/) compares the areas in more detail.

## What to pack

Kanazawa has a reputation for rain, and locals have a saying about never forgetting your umbrella, even if you forget your lunch. Pack a compact umbrella whatever the season, and see my [rainy-day guide](/articles/rainy-day-kanazawa/) for indoor plans. In winter, add waterproof shoes. Snow and slush are common, and the old streets can be slippery.

## A last tip from a local

Kanazawa looks small on a map, and it is. That is exactly why it is easy to enjoy slowly. If you can give it one night, give it one night.
