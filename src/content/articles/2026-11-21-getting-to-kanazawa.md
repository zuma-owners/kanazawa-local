**Short answer:** From **Tokyo**, take the **Hokuriku Shinkansen** straight to Kanazawa Station. The fastest trains take about **2½ hours**, with no change. From **Kyoto or Osaka**, take the **Thunderbird** limited express to **Tsuruga** and change there to the Shinkansen for the last stretch. The fastest connection from Osaka is a little over **2 hours**. From **Nagoya**, it's the **Shirasagi** to Tsuruga, then the same change.

Kanazawa used to have a direct train from Osaka. Since **March 16, 2024**, when the Shinkansen was extended from Kanazawa to Tsuruga, everyone coming from Kyoto, Osaka or Nagoya changes trains at Tsuruga. Many older blog posts still describe the old direct train, so this guide goes through the route as it works now.

*Information checked on JR West's English pages, Komatsu Airport's official site, and Japanese timetable announcements and news reports from when the line opened, on November 21, 2026. Fares and timetables change, so I don't list them here. Check a route app or the official JR sites for the day you travel.*

## How do I get from Tokyo to Kanazawa?

Take the **Hokuriku Shinkansen** (北陸新幹線, *Hokuriku Shinkansen*) from Tokyo Station. It runs all the way to Kanazawa, so you don't need to change.

There are two kinds of train on this route from Tokyo:

- **Kagayaki** (かがやき): the fastest. JR West lists **2 hours 28 minutes** as the fastest time from Kanazawa to Tokyo. Every seat on the Kagayaki is reserved. There are no non-reserved cars, so book a seat before you board.
- **Hakutaka** (はくたか): stops at more stations and takes longer. It also has non-reserved cars, which helps if your plans aren't fixed.

The Kagayaki runs mainly in the morning and the evening, so in the middle of the day you will often be on a Hakutaka. Either way, you arrive at **Kanazawa Station**. The main sights, hotels and the Loop Bus are on the **East Exit** side, where the big wooden gate is.

If you have a nationwide **Japan Rail Pass**, it covers the Hokuriku Shinkansen. You still need a seat reservation for the Kagayaki, and the top class, **Gran Class**, costs extra. Check the details of your own pass before you go.

## How do I get from Kyoto or Osaka to Kanazawa?

This is the journey that changed in 2024, and the one that confuses people most.

1. **Kyoto or Osaka → Tsuruga:** take the **Thunderbird** (サンダーバード) limited express. It leaves from Osaka Station and stops at Kyoto. Since March 2024, every Thunderbird ends at Tsuruga.
2. **Tsuruga → Kanazawa:** change to the **Hokuriku Shinkansen**. Most connecting trains are called **Tsurugi** (つるぎ), a shuttle Shinkansen between Tsuruga and Kanazawa or Toyama.

When the line opened, the fastest Osaka to Kanazawa time was reported as **2 hours 9 minutes**, including the change. From Kyoto, the time is shorter, because the Thunderbird has already covered the Osaka–Kyoto part.

Two things to know:

- **The Thunderbird is all reserved.** JR West made the Thunderbird and the Shirasagi all-reserved in March 2024. Buy a ticket with a seat, not a non-reserved one.
- **The timetable is built around the change.** The Thunderbird and the Tsurugi are timed to connect, even at night: in the timetable announced for the opening, the last Tsurugi of the day connected with the last Thunderbird and Shirasagi. You don't need to plan a long gap.

## How does the change at Tsuruga work?

It is a normal station change, not a walk across the street. The Shinkansen and the limited express trains use different floors of the same station, and you go through a **transfer gate** (乗換改札, *norikae kaisatsu*) in between. There are escalators and elevators.

JR West's English page for Tsuruga explains the gate:

- **Paper tickets:** put **all of your tickets into the gate at the same time** (usually a basic fare ticket plus the limited express and Shinkansen tickets). If you have four or more tickets, go to the staffed gate instead.
- **IC card only** (like ICOCA or Suica): touch it to pass, but this doesn't work on every section, so check before you rely on it.
- **Tickets bought from the conductor on the train:** these won't go through the automatic gate. Show them to the staff.

The word to look for on signs is **のりかえ** or **乗換** (*norikae*, "transfer"), next to **新幹線** (*Shinkansen*). If you have a big suitcase, use the elevators and don't stop to take photos on the way. The connection is timed, but it isn't long.

If you are going the other way, from Kanazawa to Kyoto or Osaka, it is the same in reverse: follow the signs for the limited express, **特急** (*tokkyū*), and the Thunderbird.

## How do I get from Nagoya to Kanazawa?

Take the **Shirasagi** (しらさぎ) limited express to Tsuruga, then change to the Shinkansen, the same way as from Osaka. Some people ride the Tokaido Shinkansen from Nagoya to **Maibara** and pick up the Shirasagi there. A route app will show you which is faster at your time of day. When the line opened, the fastest Nagoya to Kanazawa time was also reported as **2 hours 9 minutes**.

There are also daytime highway buses between Nagoya and Kanazawa, including routes run by Hokutetsu, the local bus company.

## Can I fly to Kanazawa?

Kanazawa has no airport of its own. The nearest is **Komatsu Airport** (小松空港), which has flights from **Tokyo Haneda**, among others.

From the airport, take the **Komatsu Airport Limousine Bus**. According to the airport's official page:

- It takes about **40 minutes to Kanazawa Station (West Exit)** and about **55 minutes to Korinbo** downtown.
- The bus leaves about **15 minutes after each flight arrives** (except for some flights).
- You can buy a ticket from the machine at the bus stop, or pay in cash when you get off.

Note that the bus arrives at the **West Exit**, the opposite side of the station from the wooden gate. If you're going on to Higashi Chaya or Kenrokuen, walk through the station to the East Exit for the Loop Bus. My [Kanazawa bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) shows where it leaves.

From Tokyo, flying is not usually faster than the Shinkansen once you add the trip to Haneda and the bus. It can be worth it if you are staying near Haneda or if the flight is much cheaper.

## What about night buses?

There are overnight highway buses to Kanazawa from **Tokyo** and **Osaka**, run by several companies. They take much longer than the train, usually most of the night, but they are cheaper and save a night at a hotel. Compare the options on a Japanese bus booking site or the bus company's own page. I won't recommend a specific company here.

## Do I need to book in advance?

For the **Kagayaki**, the **Thunderbird** and the **Shirasagi**, yes, you need a reserved seat, because they have no non-reserved cars. In busy times, like Golden Week (late April to early May), the Obon holidays in mid-August and the New Year, book as soon as you can.

If you are travelling in winter, also read my [winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/). Heavy snow can delay trains on the Japan Sea side, so I wouldn't plan a tight connection to a flight on the same day.

## Say it in Japanese

> **Kanazawa made, shitei-seki o onegaishimasu.** (kah-nah-zah-wah mah-deh, shee-teh-seh-kee oh oh-neh-gai-shee-mass) — "A reserved seat to Kanazawa, please." Useful at the ticket counter.

> **Tsuruga de norikae desu ka?** (tsoo-roo-gah deh noh-ree-kah-eh dess kah) — "Do I change at Tsuruga?"

> **Shinkansen no norikae wa doko desu ka?** (sheen-kahn-sen noh noh-ree-kah-eh wah doh-koh dess kah) — "Where is the transfer to the Shinkansen?"

> **Kono densha wa Kanazawa ni tomarimasu ka?** (koh-noh den-shah wah kah-nah-zah-wah nee toh-mah-ree-mass kah) — "Does this train stop at Kanazawa?"

## Before you go: a short checklist

1. **Coming from Tokyo?** Take a direct Hokuriku Shinkansen. Book a Kagayaki seat if you want the fastest train.
2. **Coming from Kyoto, Osaka or Nagoya?** Take the Thunderbird or the Shirasagi to Tsuruga, then the Shinkansen. Keep all your tickets together for the transfer gate.
3. **Flying into Komatsu?** Take the limousine bus to Kanazawa Station West Exit, about 40 minutes.
4. **Check the timetable** on a route app or the official JR site for your travel day.
