**Short answer:** Kenrokuen is open every day. From October 16 to the end of February it is open **8:00 to 17:00**, and from March 1 to October 15 it is open **7:00 to 18:00**. An adult ticket is **¥320**. Before those hours, the garden lets people in **for free** through two gates, as long as you leave before paid hours begin. If you can get up early, that is the best time to go.

Kenrokuen is the reason many people come to Kanazawa, and it deserves the reputation. It is also the place where a little planning makes the biggest difference: the same garden feels completely different at 7 a.m. and at 11 a.m.

*Information checked on the official Kenrokuen website, run by the Ishikawa Prefecture office for Kanazawa Castle and Kenrokuen, on November 17, 2026. The most detailed pages are in Japanese. Hours and fees can change, so check the [official site](https://shiro-niwa.pref.ishikawa.lg.jp/kenrokuen-garden/info.html) before you go.*

## What are Kenrokuen's opening hours?

The garden is open all year, with no regular closing day.

| Period | Opening hours | Last entry |
|---|---|---|
| March 1 – October 15 | 7:00 – 18:00 | 17:30 |
| October 16 – end of February | 8:00 – 17:00 | 16:30 |

On some evenings, mainly during cherry blossom and autumn leaf season, the garden also has night-time light-ups. Dates are announced on the official site's news and event pages, so look there if you want to see it lit up.

## How much is a ticket?

| Ticket | Price |
|---|---|
| Adult (18 and over) | ¥320 |
| Child (6 to 17) | ¥100 |
| Groups of 30 or more | ¥250 adult / ¥80 child |

Some people get in for free:

- **Visitors aged 65 and over**, if you show an official ID with your age. The official page does not limit this to Japanese residents, but bring your passport and be ready to show it.
- **Free days for everyone:** for about a week during cherry blossom season (late March to early April), three days during the Hyakumangoku Festival in early June, August 14 to 16, November 3 (Culture Day), and **December 31 to January 3**. The exact dates are announced about a week before each period.

If you are in Kanazawa over New Year, that last one is worth knowing.

## How do I pay?

You buy your ticket at the window when you go in. The official page lists a lot of options, not just cash:

- **Credit cards:** Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express, Diners Club and UnionPay
- **Transit IC cards:** Suica, ICOCA, PASMO and the other regional cards
- **Other e-money:** including PayPay, iD and QUICPay

There is also an official online ticket you can buy in advance, linked from the official page. For one ¥320 ticket, it's honestly just as easy to pay at the window.

## How does the free early-morning entry work?

This is the part most visitors miss. Before the paid hours, Kenrokuen opens for free in the early morning (the Japanese name is *sōchō kaien*, "early opening").

| Period | Free early entry | Last entry |
|---|---|---|
| March 1 – 31 | 5:00 – 6:45 | 6:30 |
| April 1 – August 31 | 4:00 – 6:45 | 6:30 |
| September 1 – October 15 | 5:00 – 6:45 | 6:30 |
| October 16 – 31 | 5:00 – 7:45 | 7:30 |
| November 1 – end of February | 6:00 – 7:45 | 7:30 |

Three rules to remember:

1. **Only two gates open early:** the **Renchimon** gate (蓮池門口) and the **Zuishinzaka** gate (随身坂口). The other gates stay closed until paid hours.
2. **You have to leave 15 minutes before paid hours begin.** In winter that means out by 7:45; in summer, by 6:45. If you want to stay longer, leave and come back in with a ticket.
3. **It is free, but it is not a separate tour.** You just walk in. Nobody checks tickets.

In winter, 6:00 is still dark, so 7:00 is a more realistic plan. You get about 45 minutes of a quiet garden, then you can buy a ticket at 8:00 if you want more time.

## What is the best time to visit?

**Early morning**, for the reasons above. Kenrokuen is beautiful, but its most famous spots are small. When the tour groups arrive, the stone lantern by the pond becomes a queue for the same photo.

If mornings are not for you, aim for **late afternoon**, an hour or two before closing. And in any season, rain is not a reason to skip it. Kanazawa gets a lot of rain, and the moss and stones look their best when wet.

**By season:**

- **Winter (November to March):** the famous *yukitsuri*, rope supports that protect the pine branches from heavy snow, go up from November 1 each year. On a snowy morning this is the Kanazawa postcard. My [winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/) explains what to wear and what changes in the city.
- **Spring:** cherry blossom season, with free days and light-ups. Also the busiest time of the year.
- **Autumn:** maples, and usually another round of light-ups.

## Which gate should I use?

The official site lists four ticket gates for Kenrokuen: **Katsurazaka** (桂坂), **Renchimon** (蓮池門), **Kodatsuno** (小立野) and **Zuishinzaka** (随身坂).

- **Katsurazaka** is the one most first-time visitors use, opposite the Ishikawa Gate of Kanazawa Castle Park. If you want to see the castle park too, this makes it easy to walk between them.
- **Kodatsuno** is flat, and it is where you can borrow a wheelchair.
- For free early entry, use **Renchimon** or **Zuishinzaka**.

Your map app will show all of them. Type the gate name with "Kenrokuen" to get directions to the right one.

## How do I get there?

By bus from Kanazawa Station, get off at **Kenrokuen-shita / Kanazawa Castle** (兼六園下・金沢城). Both directions of the Loop Bus stop there. My [guide to Kanazawa buses](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) shows which bus to take and how to pay.

For early entry, note that the Loop Bus doesn't start until 8:30. If you are staying near the station, a taxi is the easiest way to arrive by 7:00. From downtown Korinbo, it is within walking distance; a map app will give you the time from your hotel.

## What to see inside

You can see the main garden in about an hour. A few things to look for:

- **Kasumigaike pond**, the largest pond in the garden, and the **Kotoji stone lantern** with its two legs of different lengths. This is the classic photo.
- **The pine trees**, especially in winter with their rope supports.
- **Shigure-tei** (時雨亭), a teahouse inside the garden where you can sit down for tea. It is open 9:00 to 16:30 (last entry 16:00), with no entry from 12:00 to 13:00. Matcha with a seasonal sweet is ¥1,000; sencha green tea with a sweet is ¥500. It closes from December 29 to January 3.

The name *Kenrokuen* means something like "garden that combines six qualities." You don't need to know the six to enjoy it. Just walk slowly.

## Say it in Japanese

> **Otona hitori desu.** (oh-toh-nah hee-toh-ree dess) — "One adult, please." For two people: *Otona futari desu* (foo-tah-ree).

> **Kādo de ii desu ka?** (kah-doh deh ee dess kah) — "Can I pay by card?"

> **Shashin o totte moraemasu ka?** (shah-sheen oh toht-teh moh-rah-eh-mass kah) — "Could you take a photo for me?"

> **Deguchi wa doko desu ka?** (deh-goo-chee wah doh-koh dess kah) — "Where is the exit?" Useful at 7:40 in winter.

## A simple morning plan

1. Arrive at the Renchimon or Zuishinzaka gate around 7:00 (winter) for free early entry.
2. Walk to Kasumigaike pond first, before anyone else gets there.
3. Leave by 7:45, or buy a ticket at 8:00 and stay longer.
4. Walk across to **Kanazawa Castle Park**, then on to the **21st Century Museum**.
5. Lunch at **Omicho Market**.
