**Short answer:** Oyama Shrine (尾山神社, *Oyama Jinja*) is the shrine to **Maeda Toshiie**, the first lord of the Kaga domain, and his wife **Omatsu**. Its famous **Shinmon gate**, finished in 1875, has coloured glass windows on the top floor and is a national Important Cultural Property. The shrine's official site lists no admission fee, the amulet and goshuin counter is open **9:00–17:00**, and the gate is lit up from sunset until **22:00**. Give it 20 to 30 minutes, and walk on to Kanazawa Castle or Nagamachi from here.

It is one of the easiest sights to add to a day in Kanazawa, because it sits right on the walk between the castle and the [Nagamachi samurai district](/articles/nagamachi-samurai-district-guide/). Many visitors pass the bus stop without realising what is behind the trees.

*Information checked on the shrine's official site (Japanese pages) and the Kanazawa City Tourism Association's Japanese page on November 27, 2026. Times can change, so check the [official site](https://www.oyama-jinja.or.jp/) before you go.*

## What is Oyama Shrine?

The shrine was founded in **1873**, early in the Meiji era, when Toshiie's spirit was moved here to the site of a former Maeda family residence. Toshiie is the reason Kanazawa became a big castle town, so for locals this is a shrine with a strong link to the city's history. In **1998**, Omatsu, his wife, was enshrined in the main hall alongside him, so today the couple is worshipped together.

You do not need to be religious or know anything about Shinto to visit. People come to pray, to collect a goshuin stamp, or simply to look at the gate.

## Why is the gate so unusual?

The **Shinmon (神門)** is the tall gate at the front, facing the main road. It was completed in **1875**, and the shrine describes it as a mix of Japanese, Chinese and Western styles, the kind of "Western-looking" architecture built in Japan just after the country opened up.

Things to look for:

- **The coloured glass on the third floor.** The top level has windows of *giyaman* (an old word for glass). The shrine's guide says the light shines out in five colours, and that the gate once also worked as a lighthouse.
- **The lightning rod.** The shrine says the rod on the roof is said to be the **oldest surviving lightning rod in Japan**.
- **The stone.** The outer walls use *Tomuro stone* (戸室石).

The gate was named a National Treasure under the old system in 1935 and became an **Important Cultural Property** in 1950.

The best view is from the street side, looking up the steps. In the evening the gate is lit up, which is when the glass is easiest to see.

## What else is on the grounds?

The shrine's official precinct guide lists more than the gate. The ones worth a few minutes:

- **The garden (神苑).** A stroll-style pond garden behind the main hall, designated a Place of Scenic Beauty by Ishikawa Prefecture. It is said to be the last garden made by the Maeda family. Some of the islands are shaped like instruments used in *gagaku* court music. Its water was once brought over from Kenrokuen through the Tatsumi canal (辰巳用水).
- **The East Gate (東神門).** Moved here in 1963 from the Ninomaru Palace of Kanazawa Castle. It has dragon carvings, and the shrine tells a story that the palace fire spared this gate because the dragons called down water.
- **The brick fence.** The red brick fence around the shrine is said to be the first use of brick in Kanazawa, with the Maeda family's plum-blossom crest.
- **Kanaya Shrine (金谷神社).** A smaller shrine on the grounds for the later Maeda lords and their wives.
- **A statue of Toshiie,** and a "lifting stone" said to be a gift from the Maeda family.

The shrine's treasures, such as old armour and masks, are **not on public display**, so there is no museum to go into.

## Is it free, and what are the hours?

The official site lists **no admission fee** for visiting the grounds, only fees for formal prayers. It does not state hours for the grounds themselves, so plan your visit around the times it does give:

| What | Hours |
|---|---|
| Amulet counter and goshuin | 9:00–17:00, open every day |
| Gate light-up | Sunset to 22:00 (from the tourism association's page) |
| New Year | Different times, see below |

If you want a **goshuin** (a hand-written shrine stamp in a special book), go between 9:00 and 17:00. The shrine offers a regular one and paper-cut versions, including one with the gate's stained glass. The site does not list prices, so have some coins and small notes ready. My [payment guide](/articles/paying-in-kanazawa/) explains when cash still matters.

## What about New Year?

New Year is the busiest time here, as people make *hatsumōde*, the first shrine visit of the year. According to the shrine's notice for this season:

| Dates | Main hall (拝殿) | Amulet counter |
|---|---|---|
| January 1 | 0:00–21:00 | 0:00–19:00 |
| January 2–3 | 6:00–21:00 | 8:00–18:00 |
| January 4–6 | 6:00–20:00 | 8:00–18:00 |
| From January 7 | 6:00–20:00 | 9:00–17:00 |

From **December 20 to January 15**, goshuin are only available as pre-written slips (*kakioki*), not written into your book. If you are in Kanazawa over New Year, expect lines on the first three days. Dress for the cold, and see my [winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/) for what to wear.

## How do I get there?

- **By bus:** get off at **Minamicho / Oyama Shrine (南町・尾山神社)**. The shrine says it is about 3 minutes on foot. Hokutetsu city buses, the Kanazawa Loop Bus and West JR buses all stop there. My [bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) explains which buses go where.
- **By taxi:** the shrine says about 5 minutes from Kanazawa Station.
- **Address:** 11-1 Oyamamachi, Kanazawa (石川県金沢市尾山町11-1).

The shrine sits on the walk between the castle and the shopping streets of Korinbo, so you will often reach it on foot anyway.

## How do I combine it with the castle?

This is where Oyama Shrine is most useful. Right behind the shrine is the **Nezumita Gate and bridge** (鼠多門), the western entrance to [Kanazawa Castle Park](/articles/kanazawa-castle-park-guide/). That makes an easy loop:

1. **Morning:** [Kenrokuen](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/), then cross to the castle through the Ishikawa Gate.
2. **Walk through the castle** and leave by the Nezumita Gate.
3. **Oyama Shrine:** the gate, the garden and a goshuin if you collect them.
4. **Nagamachi:** walk on to the [samurai district](/articles/nagamachi-samurai-district-guide/) for the afternoon, or to [Kanazawa's evening districts](/articles/kanazawa-nightlife-areas/) later.

You can also do it in reverse in the evening: the castle is free and open until 21:00 on Saturdays, and the shrine's gate is lit until 22:00.

## Say it in Japanese

| Japanese | Say it like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 御朱印をお願いします。<br>*Goshuin o onegaishimasu.* | goh-shoo-in oh oh-neh-guy-shee-mas | A goshuin stamp, please. |
| 書き置きですか？<br>*Kakioki desu ka?* | kah-kee-oh-kee dess kah | Is it a pre-written one? |
| 写真を撮ってもいいですか？<br>*Shashin o tottemo ii desu ka?* | shah-sheen oh toh-teh-moh ee dess kah | May I take a photo? |
| 鼠多門はどこですか？<br>*Nezumita-mon wa doko desu ka?* | neh-zoo-mee-tah-mon wah doh-koh dess kah | Where is the Nezumita Gate? |

At the main hall, the usual Shinto manners are bow twice, clap twice, pray, then bow once more. Follow what the people in front of you do, and you will be fine.

## Before you go

- **No admission fee** is listed. Allow 20 to 30 minutes.
- **Goshuin and amulets:** 9:00–17:00.
- **The gate is lit up** from sunset to 22:00.
- **New Year:** longer hours, and only pre-written goshuin from Dec 20 to Jan 15.
- **Pair it with** the castle (Nezumita Gate) and Nagamachi.
