**Short answer:** Stay **near Kanazawa Station** if you have one night, a big suitcase, or an early train or Shirakawa-go bus. Stay **downtown, around Korinbo and Katamachi**, if you have two nights or more and want to walk to dinner, Kenrokuen and the nightlife. Stay **near Higashi Chaya** if a quiet, old-town evening matters more to you than convenience.

Kanazawa is a small city, and buses and taxis connect all three areas. You can't really get it wrong. But the choice changes what your evenings and mornings look like, so here is how each area feels and what to check before you book.

*I don't name or recommend hotels in this guide. Facts below were checked on the Kanazawa City Tourism Association's official site and the city's Japanese-language tax page on November 22, 2026.*

## How is Kanazawa laid out?

It helps to picture the city as a line with two ends:

- **Kanazawa Station** at the north end, where the Shinkansen, the airport bus and the highway buses arrive.
- **Downtown** to the south: **Korinbo** (香林坊, the shopping and business center) and **Katamachi** (片町, the main nightlife district) right next to it. Kenrokuen, Kanazawa Castle Park and the 21st Century Museum are close to downtown, not to the station.

Omicho Market sits between the two. **Higashi Chaya**, the old teahouse district, is off to the east, across the Asano River.

From the station to Katamachi is about ten minutes by taxi, depending on traffic, and many buses run between the station and Korinbo. So the question is not "can I get there?" but "where do I want to be at 7 a.m. and at 11 p.m.?"

## Should I stay near Kanazawa Station?

The station area is the easy, practical choice.

**Good for:**

- **Short trips.** If you arrive in the afternoon and leave the next morning, you walk off the train and into your hotel.
- **Day trips.** The highway bus to Shirakawa-go leaves from the **West Exit**, and the Komatsu Airport bus stops there too. My [Shirakawa-go guide](/articles/shirakawa-go-day-trip/) and [getting-here guide](/articles/getting-to-kanazawa/) have the details.
- **Luggage.** You never have to drag a suitcase onto a bus.
- **The Loop Bus.** It starts from platform 7 at the **East Exit**, so you can begin your sightseeing right outside. See my [bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/).

**Less good for:**

- **Evenings.** The station area has hotels and restaurants, but the real night out is downtown. The Loop Bus stops running around 6 p.m., so coming back after dinner usually means a regular city bus or a taxi.
- **Early Kenrokuen.** The garden's free early entry starts before the Loop Bus does. From the station, that means a taxi. My [Kenrokuen guide](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/) explains the early hours.

Small thing that helps: check which exit your hotel is on. The East Exit (the wooden gate) and the West Exit are on opposite sides of the station, and with a suitcase that difference matters.

## Should I stay downtown, in Korinbo or Katamachi?

Downtown is where I would point most people staying two nights or more.

**Good for:**

- **Walking.** Kenrokuen, Kanazawa Castle Park, the 21st Century Museum and the Nagamachi samurai district are all close to Korinbo. You can be in Kenrokuen for the quiet early hours without needing a bus.
- **Dinner and drinks.** Katamachi, Kiguramachi and Kakinokibatake are a short walk from Korinbo. You can stay out late and walk back. My [nightlife areas guide](/articles/kanazawa-nightlife-areas/) explains what each street is like.
- **Rainy days.** When the weather turns, which it often does here, you are close to shops, cafés and the museum instead of a bus ride away.

**Less good for:**

- **Arrival and departure days.** You need a bus or taxi between the station and your hotel, with your bags.
- **Noise.** Katamachi is lively on Friday and Saturday nights. If you sleep lightly, look at where the hotel is on the map, not just the area name. Korinbo, on the main road, is a bit calmer than the middle of Katamachi.

## What about Higashi Chaya?

The teahouse district and the streets along the Asano River are the most atmospheric part of town, especially early in the morning and after the day visitors go home. Staying nearby means you can walk the old streets when they are almost empty.

The trade-off is convenience. It's further from the station and from Katamachi, so most evenings out mean a taxi or a bus. If you book a small guesthouse or a rented townhouse here, check two things before you go:

- **How you check in**, and until what time. Small places often have set hours or a key box instead of a 24-hour front desk.
- **Whether they can take a luggage delivery** (see below). The tourism association's delivery service doesn't deliver to places without a front desk, such as whole-house rentals.

## What if I have a big suitcase?

This is where Kanazawa is better prepared than many cities.

- **Same-day delivery to your hotel.** The **Kanazawa Station Tourist Information Centre** runs a same-day baggage delivery service, part of the national "Hands-Free Travel" program. You buy a delivery ticket at the information counter, fill in a form, and leave your bags at the Hands-Free Travel counter. Your bags reach your hotel later that day. The centre is open 8:30 to 20:00, all year. You can't choose a delivery time, the cut-off time depends on where you're staying, and the counter can get busy, so go early. Valuables, fragile items and food are not accepted. Ask at the counter about the fee for your bags.
- **Coin lockers.** According to the tourism association, Kanazawa Station has nearly **900 coin lockers** in five spots, several of them open 24 hours, plus a staffed left-luggage counter in the daytime. Lockers take cash and IC cards like Suica and ICOCA. My [Kanazawa Station guide](/articles/kanazawa-station-guide/) lists where they are and what they cost.

So if you're staying downtown, you can drop your bags at the station, go straight out sightseeing, and find them waiting at your hotel in the evening.

## Is there a hotel tax in Kanazawa?

Yes. Kanazawa City charges a **lodging tax** (宿泊税, *shukuhaku-zei*) per person, per night. You pay it to the hotel with your bill. According to the city's tax page:

| Room charge per person, per night | Tax |
|---|---|
| Under ¥5,000 | None |
| ¥5,000 to under ¥20,000 | ¥200 |
| ¥20,000 or more | ¥500 |

The "under ¥5,000" exemption has applied since October 2024. Some booking sites include the tax in the price and some add it at the hotel, so don't be surprised by a small extra charge at check-out.

## So which one should I choose?

Here is the simple version I would give a friend:

1. **One night, or a day trip the next morning?** Station area.
2. **Two or three nights, and you want to eat out and walk everywhere?** Downtown, around Korinbo.
3. **Coming mainly for the old streets and a quiet evening?** Near Higashi Chaya, and plan on taxis at night.
4. **Can't decide?** Downtown, and use the station's luggage delivery on the day you arrive.

## Say it in Japanese

> **Chekku-in o onegaishimasu.** (chek-koo-een oh oh-neh-gai-shee-mass) — "I'd like to check in, please." Then give the name on your booking.

> **Nimotsu o azukatte moraemasu ka?** (nee-moh-tsoo oh ah-zoo-kaht-teh moh-rah-eh-mass kah) — "Could you keep my luggage?" Useful before check-in time or after check-out.

> **Kono hoteru made onegaishimasu.** (koh-noh hoh-teh-roo mah-deh oh-neh-gai-shee-mass) — "To this hotel, please." Show the taxi driver the hotel's name or address on your phone.

> **Chekku-auto wa nan-ji desu ka?** (chek-koo-ow-toh wah nahn-jee dess kah) — "What time is check-out?"
