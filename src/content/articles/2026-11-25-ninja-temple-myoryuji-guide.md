**Short answer:** You can only see the inside of Myoryuji (妙立寺), the "Ninja Temple", on a guided tour of about 40 minutes, and you must reserve it **by phone** on **076-241-0888** (8:30–17:00). Bookings open one month before the day you want. There is no online or email booking. The tour is in **Japanese only**, it is **cash only**, and children below elementary-school age cannot go in. Admission is ¥1,200 for adults until November 30, 2026, and **¥1,300 from December 1, 2026**.

Myoryuji is the one famous sight in Kanazawa where you cannot simply turn up. The rules are stricter than visitors expect, and several of them are written only in Japanese on the temple's site. This guide puts them in one place so that you arrive with a booking, on time, and knowing what the tour is.

*Information checked on Myoryuji's official site (Japanese and English pages) and the Kanazawa City Tourism Association's site on November 25, 2026. Rules and prices change, so check before you go.*

## Is it really a ninja temple?

No, and the temple says so itself. Its Japanese FAQ is very direct: Myoryuji is a Buddhist temple of the Nichiren school, it has nothing to do with ninja, and there are no ninja shows or ninja experiences. It is called *Ninja-dera* (忍者寺) because the building is full of tricks, like a ninja house.

The real story is better. According to the temple's own history, it was founded in **1643** on the orders of **Maeda Toshitsune**, the third lord of the Kaga domain, and moved to its present site in the 1650s. The Maeda family was one of the richest in Japan and was closely watched by the Tokugawa shogunate. The temples gathered on the far side of the Sai River were meant to work as an outer line of defence for Kanazawa Castle, and Myoryuji is said to have been at the centre of it.

That is why the inside is a maze. The tourism association describes an offering box that works as a pitfall, staircases hidden under floorboards, and a well that is said to have had an escape tunnel towards the castle. From the street the building looks like it has two storeys. Inside, it is said to have seven levels, 23 rooms and 29 staircases.

## How do I book the Ninja Temple?

By phone only. Here is how it works, from the temple's reservation page:

- **Number:** 076-241-0888 (from abroad, +81-76-241-0888).
- **Phone hours:** 8:30 to 17:00, Japan time.
- **When bookings open:** one month before the date of your visit.
- **Same-day booking:** possible if there is space, but each time slot has a fixed number of places. The temple says even weekdays are often fully booked, so call early.
- **Online or email booking:** not available.

The temple takes phone bookings in simple English. They will ask for the number of adults and children (and the children's ages or school years), your name, your nationality and how you are coming (bus, car, on foot). They will also ask you to confirm that you understand the tour is in Japanese only.

### Can my hotel book it for me?

This is the rule that catches people out. The English page just says the temple does not accept reservations "by proxy". The Japanese notice is more specific: bookings are accepted **only from the visitor in person or from a travel agency**. The temple says it will not take bookings from family members or from hotels and other accommodation, because bookings made through someone else led to too many mix-ups.

So do not count on your hotel's front desk. Make the call yourself. If you have no phone plan, an internet calling app that can dial Japanese landlines works.

## How much does it cost?

The price is changing, so check which side of the date you are on. The temple announced the change on its Japanese news page on October 1, 2026.

| | Until Nov 30, 2026 | From Dec 1, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Adults (junior high school age and up) | ¥1,200 | ¥1,300 |
| Elementary school children | ¥800 | ¥1,000 |

You pay at the entrance of the main hall when you arrive. It is **cash only**: no cards and no electronic money.

## When are the tours?

- **Hours:** tours run from 9:00 to 16:00.
- **How often:** every hour or every 30 minutes, depending on the day. The tourism association's Japanese page says hourly on weekdays and every 30 minutes on weekends and holidays, with the weekday pattern in winter. Ask for the times when you call.
- **Length:** about 40 minutes. You move through the building as a group, and you cannot leave partway except in an emergency.
- **Closed:** January 1 and 2, and days when the temple holds memorial services.

**Be on time.** Entry opens 10 minutes before your slot and the guide starts exactly on the hour. The temple is clear that if you are late, your booking is cancelled, and it does not normally change booking times. If you cannot make it, phone to cancel.

## What is the tour like if I don't speak Japanese?

Be honest with yourself about this one before you book.

- The guide speaks **Japanese only**. There are no tours in other languages.
- **Translating during the tour is not allowed**, even quietly by a friend in your group. The temple explains that other visitors complained they could not hear the guide.
- **No photos or audio recording** inside. Photos are fine in the grounds.
- **Mobile phones cannot be used inside**, so a translation app will not help you either.

You can still enjoy it. The tricks are physical: a guide lifts a floorboard and there is a staircase. If the history matters to you, read up before you go, starting with the section above.

## Who can't go in?

- **Children below elementary-school age.** The temple uses the Japanese school-entry date, including for children from abroad. For younger elementary-school children, it asks you to bring ID that shows their age, so bring their passport.
- **Wheelchair users** can enter the grounds but not the building. There are steps everywhere and 29 staircases.
- **Pets** can enter the grounds only. Assistance dogs are welcome inside.
- **Large luggage:** there are small coin lockers, but suitcases do not fit. There is a simple space to leave big bags, with no lock. It is easier to leave them at the station first; my [Kanazawa Station guide](/articles/kanazawa-station-guide/) lists the lockers.

One more thing for this winter: the temple is repairing the outer walls and roof of a separate building from autumn 2026. Tours are planned to run as normal, but the temple warns that part of the view may be covered and there may be construction noise.

## How do I get there?

The nearest bus stop is **Hirokoji** (広小路), about 3 minutes away. From Kanazawa Station's East Gate bus terminal, the temple says any bus from stands 8, 9 and 10 goes there, and so does the **left loop** of the Kanazawa Loop Bus from stand 7. The right loop does not stop at Hirokoji. The temple gives about 30 minutes by bus from the station, or about 15 minutes by taxi. My [Kanazawa bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) explains how to pay.

There is no car park, and the temple asks visitors to come by public transport, because a full car park nearby can make you miss your slot.

Myoryuji sits in the Teramachi temple district, in the same area as the Nishi Chaya district, so the two fit into one half-day. If you liked the teahouse streets, compare them with [Higashi Chaya](/articles/higashi-chaya-district-guide/) on the other side of town.

## Say it in Japanese

You can book in simple English, but a few words of Japanese make the call go smoothly.

| Japanese | Say it like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 予約をお願いします。<br>*Yoyaku o onegaishimasu.* | yoh-yah-koo oh oh-neh-guy-shee-mas | I'd like to make a reservation. |
| 英語でもいいですか？<br>*Eigo demo ii desu ka?* | ay-goh deh-moh ee dess kah | Is English OK? |
| 明日、大人二人です。<br>*Ashita, otona futari desu.* | ah-shtah, oh-toh-nah foo-tah-ree dess | Tomorrow, two adults. |
| 何時が空いていますか？<br>*Nanji ga aite imasu ka?* | nahn-jee gah eye-teh ee-mas kah | What times are free? |

Swap *ashita* (tomorrow) for *kyō* (kyoh, today) or *asatte* (ah-saht-teh, the day after tomorrow). *Hitori* is one person, *futari* two, *san-nin* three.

## Before you go

- Call **076-241-0888** yourself, 8:30–17:00, up to one month ahead.
- Bring **cash**: ¥1,200 per adult, or ¥1,300 from December 1, 2026.
- Arrive **10 minutes early**. Late means cancelled.
- No photos, no phones and no translating inside.
- Bus to **Hirokoji**, then walk about 3 minutes.
