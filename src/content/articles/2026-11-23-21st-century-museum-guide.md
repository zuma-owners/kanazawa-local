**Short answer:** You can walk into the 21st Century Museum for free. The building's public zone is open **9:00 to 22:00** and costs nothing. To see the exhibitions you need a ticket, and the exhibition zone is open **10:00 to 18:00** (until **20:00** on Fridays and Saturdays) and **closed on Mondays**. Also note that the museum will **close from May 6, 2027 until March 2028** for major repairs, so if your trip falls in that window, plan around it.

The museum is round, low and glass-walled, and it sits right between Kenrokuen and downtown Korinbo. Almost everyone who visits Kanazawa passes it, and plenty of them walk in, wander for ten minutes and leave unsure what they were allowed to see. This guide is here to fix that.

*Information checked on the museum's official website, in English and Japanese, on November 23, 2026. Hours, exhibitions and prices change, so check the [official site](https://www.kanazawa21.jp/en/) before you go.*

## What is free at the 21st Century Museum?

The museum is split into two parts:

- **The public zone** (交流ゾーン, *kōryū zōn*): free, no ticket needed. The museum's Japanese guide says it plainly: entering the building itself is free. This is where you'll find the café, the shop, the library and the corridors that circle the building. It is open 9:00 to 22:00 and only closes over New Year.
- **The exhibition zone** (展覧会ゾーン, *tenrankai zōn*): ticketed. This covers the galleries with the paid exhibition and the permanent works inside it.

A few other free things worth knowing:

- **Some exhibitions are free.** When I checked, the museum's site listed two free exhibitions running until April 18, 2027, alongside the paid one. Look for the "Free" label on the official site.
- **Free Wi-Fi** in the public zone.
- **The outside.** The museum's floor map shows some permanent works outside, on the lawn around the building, so a walk around the museum is worth a few minutes too.

So even on a Monday, or if you have only twenty minutes, it is worth going in.

## What are the opening hours?

| Area | Hours |
|---|---|
| Exhibition zone (ticketed) | 10:00 – 18:00, until 20:00 on Fridays and Saturdays |
| Public zone (free) | 9:00 – 22:00 |

Tickets stop being sold 30 minutes before closing. The café, shop and library each have their own hours, all inside the times above.

## Is the 21st Century Museum closed on Mondays?

The **exhibition zone** is closed on Mondays. When a Monday is a national holiday, it opens that Monday and closes the **next weekday** instead. This catches out a lot of visitors, because Japan has many Monday holidays.

For this winter, the official exhibition page lists these exceptions:

- **Open** on Monday November 23, 2026, January 11, 2027 and March 22, 2027 (all holidays)
- **Closed** on the following days instead: November 24, January 12 and March 23

**New Year:** the whole museum, free zone included, is closed from **December 29, 2026 to January 1, 2027**. It reopens on January 2 and 3, with the exhibitions closing early at 17:00 on those two days.

One honest tip: when I checked, the closing-day calendar on the museum's English visitor page was out of date, still showing dates up to March 2026. The **Japanese page** had the current calendar, up to March 2027. If you're checking a specific date, look at the Japanese page too (your browser can translate it).

## How much is a ticket, and where do I buy it?

There is no single fixed price. Each paid exhibition sets its own. For the current exhibition, *From Today to Tomorrow: On the Stewardship of a Work* (October 31, 2026 to April 18, 2027), the official prices are:

| Ticket | At the museum | Online / groups of 20+ |
|---|---|---|
| Adult | ¥1,200 | ¥1,000 |
| University student | ¥800 | ¥600 |
| School student | ¥400 | ¥300 |
| 65 and over | ¥1,000 | — |

Preschool children are free. The museum calls this its final special exhibition before the long closure.

You can buy tickets in two ways:

- **At the museum,** on the day. The Japanese page says same-day counter sales start at 9:30.
- **Online,** through the ticket link on the official site. For special exhibitions, the online tickets are for a set date and time.

Buying online is cheaper and means less waiting at busy times. If your plans are loose, the counter is fine.

## How does The Swimming Pool work?

Leandro Erlich's *The Swimming Pool* is the work most visitors come for: from above it looks like a pool full of water, and from below you can stand "under" the water and look up at the people looking down.

To go **underneath**, you need two things:

1. **A ticket** for the exhibition. The entrance to the underground space is inside the collection exhibition area.
2. **A same-day waiting number.** You can get one from 9:00 to 17:30 (until 19:30 on Fridays and Saturdays), either from a machine at the general information desk or online through the link on the museum's Swimming Pool page. The online one shows your place in the queue on your phone.

There are **no advance reservations**. You get the number on the day you visit. When you're called, go to Exhibition Room 6. Each visit underground is limited to **5 minutes**, and if you are late after being called, your place is cancelled. Numbers can run out on busy days, so get yours early, then go and see the rest of the museum while you wait.

The official pages do not say clearly whether looking at the pool **from above** needs a ticket. Ask at the information desk if it matters for your plans.

## Are there lockers?

Yes. The museum has **coin lockers in four places** in the building. They take a ¥100 coin, and you get it back when you open the locker. If your suitcase is too big, the museum says staff may be able to keep it at the information and ticket desk. There is also a ¥100 umbrella stand, which also gives your coin back, and in Kanazawa you will probably need it.

## The 2027–2028 closure

The museum has announced that it will **close from May 6, 2027 to March 2028** for equipment upgrades and repair work. If you visit Kanazawa in that period, the building will not be open, so check the official site closer to the time for any news.

## How do I get there?

The nearest bus stop is **Hirosaka / 21st Century Museum** (広坂・21世紀美術館, *Hirosaka Nijūisseiki Bijutsukan*), about a minute's walk away. From Kanazawa Station's East Exit, it's roughly 10 minutes by local bus, or about 20 minutes on the Loop Bus. My [guide to riding the bus in Kanazawa](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) explains which stands to use and how to pay.

It is a short walk from Kenrokuen, so most people pair the two. Do the garden early, when it is quiet (see my [Kenrokuen guide](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/) for free early entry), then walk over for the museum when it opens at 10:00. My [first-timer's itinerary](/articles/kanazawa-first-time-itinerary/) builds a whole day around that.

## Say it in Japanese

| Japanese | Say it like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 大人二枚お願いします。<br>*Otona ni-mai onegaishimasu.* | oh-toh-nah nee-my oh-neh-guy-shee-mas | Two adult tickets, please. |
| コインロッカーはどこですか？<br>*Koin rokkā wa doko desu ka?* | koh-een rock-kah wah doh-koh dess kah | Where are the coin lockers? |
| プールの整理券はまだありますか？<br>*Pūru no seiriken wa mada arimasu ka?* | poo-roo noh say-ree-ken wah mah-dah ah-ree-mas kah | Are there still numbers for the Pool? |
| 今日は開いていますか？<br>*Kyō wa aite imasu ka?* | kyoh wah eye-teh ee-mas kah | Is it open today? |

*Seiriken* (整理券) means a numbered ticket for a queue. It is a useful word all over Japan, not only at museums.

## Before you go

- Check the date: Monday, holiday, New Year, or after May 6, 2027?
- If you want The Swimming Pool, get your waiting number early in the day.
- Bring a ¥100 coin for the lockers and the umbrella stand.
- On a day off or a rainy day, the free zone is still worth an hour.
