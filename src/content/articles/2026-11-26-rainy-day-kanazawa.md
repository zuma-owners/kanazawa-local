**Short answer:** A rainy day in Kanazawa doesn't have to be a wasted one. The city's best indoor sights sit within walking distance of each other around the 21st Century Museum: the **21st Century Museum** itself, the **D.T. Suzuki Museum**, the **National Crafts Museum** and the **Ishikawa Prefectural Museum of Art**. Add covered **Omicho Market** for lunch and you have a full day under a roof. Before you plan it, check the day of the week. Several of these museums **close on Mondays**, and the gold leaf museum closes on **Tuesdays**.

Rain is normal here, not bad luck. The Japan Meteorological Agency's 1991–2020 averages for Kanazawa give about **2,400 mm of rain and snow a year**, with **177 days** of at least 1 mm. That is roughly one day in two. Locals even have a saying for it, *bentō wasuretemo, kasa wasureruna*: "forget your lunch if you must, but never forget your umbrella."

*Information checked on each museum's official site (Japanese pages) and the Japan Meteorological Agency's climate data on November 26, 2026. Hours, fees and closed days change, so check the official sites before you go.*

## Does it really rain that much in Kanazawa?

Yes. Kanazawa is on the Sea of Japan side of the country, and in late autumn and winter cold air crossing the sea brings grey skies, drizzle and wet snow. My [winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/) explains what that means for clothes and shoes.

The practical takeaway: if your trip is two or three days long, assume at least one of them will be wet. Plan an indoor day you can swap in, and keep the outdoor sights flexible.

## Which indoor sights are close together?

The four museums below are all in the green area between Kenrokuen and downtown, so you can walk between them with an umbrella instead of waiting for buses. Here is what their official pages say.

| Museum | Hours (last entry) | Closed | Adult ticket |
|---|---|---|---|
| **21st Century Museum** (金沢21世紀美術館) | Free zone 9:00–22:00. Exhibitions 10:00–18:00 (to 20:00 Fri–Sat) | Exhibitions closed **Mondays** | Depends on the exhibition |
| **D.T. Suzuki Museum** (鈴木大拙館) | 9:30–17:00 (16:30) | **Mondays** | ¥310 |
| **National Crafts Museum** (国立工芸館) | 9:30–17:30 (17:00) | **Mondays** | Depends on the exhibition |
| **Ishikawa Prefectural Museum of Art** (石川県立美術館) | 9:30–18:00 (17:30) | No weekly closing day | ¥370 for the collection |

When a Monday is a national holiday, the Monday museums open that day and close the next weekday instead. All of them close over New Year, and each closes for a few days at a time when exhibitions change, so check the dates for your trip.

### 21st Century Museum

The round glass building is the easy first stop, because **the public zone is free** and open late. You can walk in, see some of the works and get out of the rain without a ticket. For the exhibitions, Monday closures and the long closure that starts in May 2027, see my [21st Century Museum guide](/articles/21st-century-museum-guide/).

### D.T. Suzuki Museum

A small, quiet museum about D.T. Suzuki, the Kanazawa-born writer who introduced Zen Buddhism to readers in the West. It is small and quiet, which makes it a calm break on a wet afternoon. Adults pay **¥310**. Over-65s pay ¥210 and high school students and younger are free. It is closed on **Mondays** and from **December 29 to January 3**.

### National Crafts Museum

Japan's national museum of modern crafts moved to Kanazawa from Tokyo. According to its site, the museum moved and rebuilt two **Meiji-era Western-style buildings**: the former headquarters of the army's 9th Division (1898) and the former army officers' club (1909). The exhibitions change through the year, and so does the price. For the current exhibition, running until **January 11, 2027**, adults pay **¥1,200** and university students ¥800. Under-18s are free. Closed on **Mondays**, plus exhibition changes and New Year.

### Ishikawa Prefectural Museum of Art

The prefecture's own art museum stands next to the National Crafts Museum. It has **no regular weekly closing day**, which makes it a useful plan B on a Monday. The collection galleries cost **¥370** for adults (¥290 for over-65s and university students). Special exhibitions have their own prices. Two things to know: the galleries close for exhibition changes (on the official calendar for this winter: **December 8–11, 2026**, **December 29, 2026 to January 3, 2027**, and **January 25 to February 10, 2027**), and sometimes only one of the two floors is open.

## What about a rainy Monday?

Monday is the hard day. The 21st Century Museum's exhibitions, the D.T. Suzuki Museum and the National Crafts Museum are all closed. What still works:

- **21st Century Museum free zone**, open 9:00 to 22:00 even on Mondays.
- **Ishikawa Prefectural Museum of Art**, if it isn't between exhibitions.
- **Kanazawa Yasue Gold Leaf Museum** (金沢市立安江金箔工芸館), the city museum about Kanazawa's gold leaf craft. It closes on **Tuesdays**, not Mondays. Open 9:30 to 17:00 (last entry 16:30), adults **¥310**. It also closes for exhibition changes, including **December 7–11, 2026**.
- **Omicho Market** (see below).

If you are planning the rest of your trip around the weather, my [first-timer's itinerary](/articles/kanazawa-first-time-itinerary/) has one-, two- and three-day plans you can shuffle.

## Where can you eat without getting soaked?

**Omicho Market** (近江町市場) is a covered market of about 170 shops in narrow lanes, so once you are inside you can walk around under a roof. Every shop sets its own hours, the lunch lines get long, and some stalls sell out and close early, so go in the morning or early in the day. My [Omicho Market guide](/articles/omicho-market-guide/) covers timing and what to eat.

**Kanazawa Station** is the other easy option, with shops and restaurants inside the station building. If you are arriving or leaving on a wet day, my [station guide](/articles/kanazawa-station-guide/) explains the exits, lockers and where the buses leave.

For a warm dinner after a cold, wet day, [Kanazawa oden](/articles/kanazawa-oden-guide/) is exactly what locals reach for.

## Should you still go to Kenrokuen in the rain?

In light rain, yes. The garden is outdoors with gravel paths, so you will get wet, but the moss and stones look their best when wet, and there are usually fewer people. Heavy rain is different. If it is pouring, swap days and go to the museums first. My [Kenrokuen guide](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/) has the hours and the free early entry.

The same goes for the old streets: [Higashi Chaya](/articles/higashi-chaya-district-guide/) and [Nagamachi](/articles/nagamachi-samurai-district-guide/) are lovely in drizzle, and the stone and wood darken nicely. But you are walking outside the whole time, so save them for a lighter spell.

## Umbrellas: what to know

- **Buy one when you arrive** if you didn't pack one. Clear plastic umbrellas, called *binīru-gasa* (ビニール傘), are sold at convenience stores.
- **Use the umbrella stand or bag at the door.** Many shops, museums and restaurants have an umbrella stand (*kasa-tate*, 傘立て) or thin plastic sleeves at the entrance. Put your wet umbrella in one before you walk in.
- **Waterproof shoes** matter more than a raincoat in late autumn and winter. The streets get wet and, in winter, slushy.

## Say it in Japanese

| Japanese | Say it like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 傘はどこで買えますか？<br>*Kasa wa doko de kaemasu ka?* | kah-sah wah doh-koh deh kah-eh-mass kah | Where can I buy an umbrella? |
| 今日は開いていますか？<br>*Kyō wa aite imasu ka?* | kyoh wah eye-teh ee-mass kah | Are you open today? |
| 傘はここに置いてもいいですか？<br>*Kasa wa koko ni oitemo ii desu ka?* | kah-sah wah koh-koh nee oy-teh-moh ee dess kah | Can I leave my umbrella here? |
| よく降りますね。<br>*Yoku furimasu ne.* | yoh-koo foo-ree-mass neh | It really keeps raining, doesn't it? |

The last one is small talk. Say it to a shop owner or the person next to you at a counter and you will probably get a smile and a story about Kanazawa weather.

## Rainy-day checklist

- About **one day in two** has rain in Kanazawa. Plan an indoor day you can swap in.
- **Mondays:** the D.T. Suzuki Museum, the National Crafts Museum and the 21st Century Museum's exhibitions are closed. The Prefectural Museum of Art and the 21st Century Museum's free zone are your best bets.
- **Tuesdays:** the gold leaf museum is closed.
- Check **exhibition-change closures** and **New Year** dates on each museum's site.
- Lunch under a roof at **Omicho Market**, dinner of oden.
- Carry an umbrella, wear waterproof shoes, and use the umbrella stand at the door.
- Want to leave the city? A hot-spring bath works in any weather: see the [Kaga Onsen day trip](/articles/kaga-onsen-day-trip/).
