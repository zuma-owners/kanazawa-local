**Short answer:** Take the highway bus from the **West Exit of Kanazawa Station**. It takes about **1 hour 15 to 1 hour 25 minutes** and costs **¥2,900 one way from December 1, 2026** (¥2,800 until November 30). Some buses need a reservation, some don't, and bookings open one month ahead. Leave your suitcase in Kanazawa.

Shirakawa-go is the most popular day trip from Kanazawa, and it is easy to do on your own. The thatched farmhouses are real, and the village is small enough to see in a few hours. What catches people out is not the village itself but the logistics: the bus sells out on busy days, big suitcases are a problem, and the winter light-up evenings work very differently from a normal day.

*Information checked on the official Nohi Bus timetable page and the Shirakawa-go Tourist Association website on November 18, 2026. The detailed timetable, luggage rules and light-up rules are on the Japanese pages; the English bus page was not available when I checked. Times and fares change, so look at the [official timetable](https://www.nouhibus.co.jp/route_bus/shirakawago/) before you go.*

## How do I get from Kanazawa to Shirakawa-go?

By highway bus. There is no train to Shirakawa-go.

- **Where it leaves:** Kanazawa Station, **West Exit** (金沢駅西口, *Kanazawa-eki nishi-guchi*). Note that this is the west side, not the side with the big wooden gate.
- **Who runs it:** several bus companies share the route, including Nohi Bus and Hokutetsu (Hokuriku Railroad), so the bus may not have the company name you expect. What matters is the destination: 白川郷 (Shirakawa-gō).
- **How long:** about 1 hour 15 to 1 hour 25 minutes on the current timetable. Some buses stop at **Gokayama Sugenuma**, another thatched-roof village, but the timetable says that stop is served only from April 1 to November 30.
- **Where you arrive:** the **Shirakawa-go Bus Terminal**, a short walk from the village. The tourist association's office is inside the terminal.

There are several departures a day in each direction. **The timetable and fares change on December 1, 2026**, so if you are travelling in December or later, use the new timetable on the official page, not an older blog post or screenshot.

## Do I need to book the bus?

Sometimes. On the official timetable, some buses are marked **予約制** (*yoyaku-sei*, "reservation required"). Others take no reservations: you just get on.

- **Reservations open one month before** the travel date.
- **Online:** through *Hassha Oh-Line Net* (発車オ～ライネット), the Japanese bus booking site linked from the Nohi Bus timetable page.
- **By phone:** Nohi Bus reservation centre, 0577-32-1688 (9:00 to 17:00), or Hokutetsu, 076-234-0123 (9:00 to 18:00).
- For buses without reservations, the official page also links to a **mobile ticket** you can buy in advance.

My advice: if your date is fixed, book a reserved bus for the way there and the way back. Shirakawa-go is a day where you do not want to stand at the station hoping for a seat, especially in autumn, on weekends, and in snow season.

## How much does it cost?

The official fare table lists Kanazawa Station West Exit to Shirakawa-go at **¥2,800 one way until November 30, 2026**, and **¥2,900 from December 1, 2026**. A return trip is double that. Check the official page for children's fares and any changes.

## What should I do with my luggage?

**Leave it in Kanazawa.** This is the most common mistake on this trip.

The bus has a luggage space underneath, but the official page says space is limited and **large bags may be refused**. Inside the bus, you are responsible for your own bags, and heavy items should not go on the overhead rack.

At the village end, I could not find official information about luggage storage on the tourist association's site. So don't count on finding a place to leave a big suitcase there. If you are checking out of your hotel that day, ask the hotel to keep your bag, or put it in a locker at Kanazawa Station before you get on the bus. Then travel with a small daypack.

If you are **moving on to Takayama** instead of coming back, the same route continues there (the official page says about 50 minutes from Shirakawa-go to Takayama). In that case you will have your bags with you, so book a reserved bus and pack as light as you can.

## What is there to do in Shirakawa-go?

The main village is called **Ogimachi** (荻町). Most people spend **three to four hours** there, which is about right for a day trip.

- **Walk the village.** The farmhouses with steep thatched roofs are called *gasshō-zukuri*, because the roofs look like hands pressed together in prayer (*gasshō*). Some houses are open to visitors as small museums; most are not.
- **Go up to the viewpoint.** The classic photo of the whole village is from the **Ogimachi Castle Ruins Viewpoint** (荻町城跡展望台, *Ogimachi shiroato tenbōdai*). The official site says it is about 15 to 20 minutes on foot from the path behind the Wada House, and there is also a shuttle bus. Check the shuttle times on the day.
- **Eat lunch in the village** and plan your bus back in the mid-afternoon.

## What are the rules in the village?

Remember that Shirakawa-go is not a theme park. People live in these houses. The tourist association describes the World Heritage village as a residential area, and its notices are clear on a few points:

- **No drones.** Drone use is prohibited.
- **No tripods at the viewpoint.**
- **No parking or dropping people off outside the official car parks** (this matters if you rent a car).

Beyond that, use common sense: don't walk into gardens or open doors to look inside, and take your rubbish back with you.

## What about the winter light-up?

On a few winter evenings, the village is lit up at night. It is beautiful, and it is also the most complicated part of this trip.

For 2027, the official dates are **January 11, 17, 24 and 31**, from **17:30 to 19:30**. The tourist association says the event is **fully pre-booked**, with **no same-day tickets**. In practice that means visitors come on a light-up tour, a hotel stay with tickets, or with a pre-booked parking space.

If you are going on a light-up day by regular bus **without** a ticket, know this:

- The regular buses back to Kanazawa leave **before** the light-up starts.
- The official notice asks people on route buses to arrive by around **15:20**, and the gate closes at **15:30** for anyone without a ticket.
- The notice says taxis from Shirakawa do not operate on light-up days.

So if you want a quiet day visit, pick a different date. If you want the light-up, look at the official tourist association site now. Most tour and hotel sales for 2027 have already opened.

## Is it OK to go in winter?

Yes, and snow is when Shirakawa-go looks most like the postcards. But plan for two things:

1. **Delays.** The official bus page warns that snow, parking queues at Shirakawa-go or highway closures can delay buses by **two hours or more**. Don't book a train or flight out of Kanazawa the same evening.
2. **Cold feet.** The village is colder and snowier than Kanazawa. My [winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/) covers what to wear and which shoes work on snow.

If you are driving, the tourist association says winter tyres (studless tyres) are a must.

## Say it in Japanese

> **Kono basu wa Shirakawa-gō ni ikimasu ka?** (koh-noh bah-soo wah shee-rah-kah-wah-goh nee ee-kee-mass kah) — "Does this bus go to Shirakawa-go?"

> **Yoyaku shite imasu.** (yoh-yah-koo shteh ee-mass) — "I have a reservation." Show your booking on your phone at the same time.

> **Nimotsu o shita ni iretemo ii desu ka?** (nee-moh-tsoo oh shee-tah nee ee-reh-teh-moh ee dess kah) — "Can I put my bag underneath?"

> **Kaeri no basu wa nanji desu ka?** (kah-eh-ree noh bah-soo wah nahn-jee dess kah) — "What time is the bus back?"

## A simple day plan

1. Leave your big bags at your hotel or in a locker at Kanazawa Station.
2. Take a morning bus from the **West Exit**, reserved if you can.
3. Walk the village first, then go up to the viewpoint.
4. Lunch in the village.
5. Take a mid-afternoon bus back, and have dinner in Kanazawa. My [izakaya guide](/articles/izakaya-english-kanazawa/) will help with that.

If you only have one or two days in Kanazawa, a Shirakawa-go trip takes most of one of them.
