**Short answer:** Omicho Market is open roughly **9:00 to 17:00**, but every shop sets its own hours and days off, and the market closes for the New Year holidays. Go **in the morning**, ideally before 11:00, when the stalls are full and the lunch lines haven't started. Eat **seafood**: a rice bowl topped with raw fish (*kaisendon*), or whatever the stalls are grilling that day. In winter, that means crab.

Omicho (近江町, *oh-mee-choh*) is Kanazawa's main fresh food market, and locals call it "Kanazawa's kitchen." It is on almost every first-time plan, including [mine](/articles/kanazawa-first-time-itinerary/). It is also easy to arrive at the wrong time, wander for twenty minutes, and leave without eating anything. Here is how to avoid that.

*Information checked on the Omicho Market Promotion Association's website, its 2026 Japanese map and its English pamphlet, plus Visit Kanazawa and Ishikawa Travel, on November 19, 2026. The market's own site is mostly in Japanese. Hours change, so check the [official site](https://ohmicho-ichiba.com/) before you go.*

## What is Omicho Market?

A covered market of about **170 shops** in narrow lanes between Kanazawa Station and downtown. The market traces its history back about **300 years**: according to its own pamphlet, Kanazawa's markets came together on this spot in the early 1700s after a series of fires in the castle town, and the prefecture officially recognized it as a public market in 1904.

Today it is a retail market, not a wholesale one, so you can buy as little as you like. Alongside the fish shops there are vegetable and fruit sellers, butchers, pickle and fish-cake makers, sweet shops, florists, and a lot of small restaurants.

## What are the opening hours?

| | Hours (official 2026 map) |
|---|---|
| Market shops (general) | 9:00 – 17:00 |
| Omicho Ichiba-kan building, basement floor (food and daily goods) | 9:00 – 20:00 |
| Omicho Ichiba-kan building, 2nd floor (restaurants) | 11:00 – 23:00 |

Three things to know:

1. **The hours are a guide, not a rule.** Every shop decides its own opening times and days off. Some restaurants open earlier than 9:00, and some stalls sell out and close early.
2. **There is no single weekly closing day for the whole market.** Different shops close on different days, and some post their closures on the market's website. If you have your heart set on one place, check it before you go.
3. **The market closes for the New Year holidays.** The official pamphlet lists the New Year's sale on January 5.

## When is the best time to go?

**Between about 9:00 and 11:00.** By 9:00 most stalls are open and fully stocked. Ishikawa Travel notes that locals do their shopping early in the morning, and that at lunchtime the restaurants get busy and the popular ones have lines.

So my suggestion is simple: make it a late breakfast or an early lunch. If you arrive at 12:30 on a Saturday, expect to queue. If you arrive at 16:30, expect empty trays and stalls packing up.

**By season:**

- **Winter (November to March)** is the market's big season. Snow crab fishing in Ishikawa opens on November 6, and the market fills with crab. Visit Kanazawa says it gets crowded with both tourists and locals at this time of year. My [guide to crab season](/articles/kanazawa-crab-season/) explains the two kinds of crab and when each is available.
- **The rest of the year**, the market's own seasonal calendar includes yellowtail and cod in midwinter, bamboo shoots in spring, rock oysters in summer, and deep-water shrimp in autumn.

## What should I eat?

I don't name restaurants in guides like this one. I only recommend places I have visited and that have agreed to be featured. But these are the things to look for:

- **Kaisendon (海鮮丼):** a bowl of rice topped with raw fish and seafood. This is the classic Omicho meal. Many restaurants show photos and prices outside, so you can compare before you sit down.
- **Grilled seafood from the stalls:** Ishikawa Travel notes that some shops grill oysters, squid, crab and shrimp right at the counter for shoppers to try.
- **Crab, in season:** whole boiled crabs for sale, and crab on restaurant menus.
- **Fruit:** Ishikawa's famous Ruby Roman grapes are on the market's own seasonal list for September and October.
- **Kaga vegetables:** fifteen traditional local vegetable varieties. You probably won't cook them on your trip, but they are worth a look, and they turn up on menus all over the city.

## How does eating at the market work?

Omicho is a working market where locals shop, not a food court. A few simple habits help:

- **Eat at the stall.** If a stall grills or cuts something for you, it's polite to eat it there rather than walk down the lanes with it. The lanes are narrow, and people are carrying shopping bags.
- **Ask before you touch.** Staff are happy to show you a fish or a piece of fruit.
- **Ask questions.** The market's pamphlet lists "face-to-face service" and "experts you can ask anything" among its six charms. Asking what is good today is the normal way to shop here, and a good chance to use a little Japanese.
- **Bring some cash.** Each shop decides how you can pay, and I couldn't find any market-wide information on cards. Having cash means you won't get stuck at a small stall. The official map shows ATMs in the market.

## Lockers, restrooms and parking

The official 2026 map shows:

- **Coin lockers, including large ones**, in the basement of the **Omicho Ichiba-kan** building.
- **Restrooms** in the Omicho Ichiba-kan building and in the **Omicho Fureai-kan** building.
- **Three car parks** connected directly to the market.

So if you arrive with a big bag, you don't have to drag it through the lanes. Locker sizes and numbers are limited, so don't count on a free one at busy times.

## How do I get there?

- **On foot:** about 15 minutes from Kanazawa Station, according to the market.
- **By bus:** about 10 minutes. Get off at **Musashigatsuji / Omicho Market** (武蔵ヶ辻・近江町市場); the market is right by the stop. Both directions of the Loop Bus stop there, and my [Kanazawa bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) explains which bus to take and how to pay.

Omicho works well as lunch between a morning at [Kenrokuen](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/) and an afternoon somewhere else.

## Say it in Japanese

> **Kyō no osusume wa nan desu ka?** (kyoh noh oh-soo-soo-meh wah nahn dess kah) — "What do you recommend today?" The single most useful sentence at a market.

> **Kore, hitotsu kudasai.** (koh-reh, hee-toh-tsoo koo-dah-sigh) — "One of these, please." Point as you say it. For two: *futatsu* (foo-tah-tsoo).

> **Koko de taberaremasu ka?** (koh-koh deh tah-beh-rah-reh-mass kah) — "Can I eat it here?"

> **Jimoto no desu ka?** (jee-moh-toh noh dess kah) — "Is it local?" Useful in crab season.

## A simple Omicho plan

1. Arrive between 9:00 and 10:00. Put big bags in a locker in the Omicho Ichiba-kan basement.
2. Walk the lanes once without buying anything. See what's in season and what is being grilled.
3. Pick a kaisendon restaurant before 11:00, or graze at the stalls.
4. Take the bus on to your next stop.
