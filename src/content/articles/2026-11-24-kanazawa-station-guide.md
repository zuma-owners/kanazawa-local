**Short answer:** Kanazawa Station has two sides. The **Kenrokuen Exit (East Exit)** is the one with the big wooden gate, the Loop Bus and the taxis for the old town. The **Kanazawa Port Exit (West Exit)** is where the Shirakawa-go and Komatsu Airport buses stop. The station has **892 coin lockers** in five places (¥500 to ¥1,100 a day), a staffed left-luggage counter, and a **Tourist Information Center open 8:30 to 20:00 every day** with help in English.

Almost every trip to Kanazawa starts and ends here, and the station is easy once you know which side you need.

*Information checked on the Kanazawa City Tourism Association's official site (Visit Kanazawa) and the station shopping mall's Japanese-language official site on November 24, 2026. Hours and prices change, so check before you go.*

## Which exit do I need?

There are only two, and they are at opposite ends of one straight concourse.

| Exit | Japanese on the signs | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Kenrokuen Exit** (East Exit) | 兼六園口 (東口) | The wooden gate and glass dome, the Loop Bus, city buses, taxis to Kenrokuen, Higashi Chaya, Omicho Market and downtown |
| **Kanazawa Port Exit** (West Exit) | 金沢港口 (西口) | The highway bus to Shirakawa-go, the Komatsu Airport bus, taxis toward the port and cruise terminal |

You will hear both sets of names: "East Exit" and "Kenrokuen Exit" are the same place, and so are "West Exit" and "Kanazawa Port Exit". If you remember one thing: **the wooden gate is the sightseeing side.** Check which side your hotel is on before you arrive.

## Where can I leave my luggage?

You have four options. The details are from the tourism association's page on luggage at the station.

### Coin lockers

There are **892 coin lockers in five locations**, inside the station and its shopping areas, which are called **Anto** and **Rinto**:

| Locker area | Where | Hours | Lockers |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Anto, east entrance, next to the left-luggage counter | 24 hours | 88 |
| B | Anto, west entrance | 24 hours | 369 |
| C | South side of Anto, on the east–west passage | 24 hours | 344 |
| D | Inside Anto, next to Yamato Transport | 8:30–22:00 | 36 |
| F | Inside Rinto, next to Nippon Travel Agency | 5:30–22:00 | 55 |

- **Fees per day:** small ¥500, medium ¥700, large ¥1,000, extra large ¥1,100.
- **Payment:** cash, or a Japanese transport IC card such as Suica or ICOCA.

If you need your bag back late at night or before an early train, use A, B or C. The other two close at 22:00.

### The staffed left-luggage counter

If the lockers are full or your bag is too big, go to the **left-luggage counter at the east entrance of Anto**, near the JR ticket office. It is open **9:30 to 19:00**. Bags that would fit in a locker cost **¥600 a day**, and oversized items cost **¥1,200**. It also takes things like ski equipment, strollers and bicycles. You can't reserve, so remember the 19:00 closing time when you plan your pick-up.

### Same-day delivery to your hotel

The Tourist Information Center runs a paid same-day delivery service to hotels and ryokan in Kanazawa, and also to the Noto area, the Kaga hot spring towns and Awara in Fukui. I explained how it works, and what it won't deliver, in my [where to stay guide](/articles/where-to-stay-kanazawa/).

### Storage you can book ahead

The tourism association also lists two reservation services: **ecbo cloak**, at a travel agency inside Rinto (10:00–17:00, closed Thursdays, ¥1,000 per item, booked and paid online), and **Bounce**, which uses partner shops around the station and the sightseeing areas.

## Where is the Tourist Information Center, and what can it do?

It is inside the station, and it is the best first stop if anything about your trip is unclear.

- **Hours:** 8:30–20:00, every day of the year. English-language help from the volunteer guide service runs 8:30–19:00.
- **What you can get:** city maps and leaflets in several languages, advice on sights, transport, hotels and restaurants, and directions for onward trips to places like Shirakawa-go, Takayama, Noto and Kaga.
- **What you can buy or arrange:** the **1-day bus pass** and the same-day luggage delivery.

## Where do the buses and taxis leave from?

**Kenrokuen Exit (East):**

- The **Kanazawa Loop Bus** for the main sights leaves from **platform 7**. One ride is ¥220, and the 1-day pass is ¥800. My [Kanazawa bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) has the routes and a table of which stop to use for each sight.
- The **taxi stand** is under a roof to your right as you come out. The tourism association gives these rough fares from the station: Omicho Market from about ¥800 (around 7 minutes), Higashi Chaya from about ¥1,600 (around 11 minutes), and Kenrokuen from about ¥1,800 (around 15 minutes). Late-night rides cost more.
- **On foot,** Omicho Market is about 15 minutes away.

**Kanazawa Port Exit (West):**

- The **highway bus to Shirakawa-go** and the **Komatsu Airport limousine bus** use this side. See my [Shirakawa-go guide](/articles/shirakawa-go-day-trip/) and [how to get to Kanazawa](/articles/getting-to-kanazawa/).
- The Hokutetsu bus centre at the West Exit bus terminal also sells the 1-day pass.
- There is a second taxi stand here, better for the port and the cruise terminal.

## Where can I get cash?

The tourism association lists three places at the station:

- A **Travelex** currency exchange counter at the Nippon Travel Agency branch facing the concourse.
- An **ATM in the 7-Eleven** facing the concourse.
- A **Japan Post Bank ATM** inside Anto.

The association points visitors to 7-Eleven and Japan Post Bank ATMs for withdrawing money with overseas cards. Some small restaurants and bars still take cash only, so getting some here saves you a search later.

## Can I eat and shop at the station?

Yes. The shopping mall inside the station is **Kanazawa Hyakubangai** (金沢百番街), and it has three parts. Its official site is in Japanese, and this is what it lists:

| Zone | What it is | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| **Anto** (あんと) | Souvenirs, sweets, local food, crafts and restaurants | Shops 8:30–20:00, restaurants 11:00–22:00 |
| **Rinto** | A separate shopping zone | 10:00–20:00 |
| **Anto West** (あんと西) | A third, smaller zone | Differs by shop |

Some shops keep their own hours. Anto is where you buy sweets, sake and take-away sushi or bento before a train. Its shops close at 20:00, so don't leave souvenirs for a late departure.

## What is the big wooden gate?

Outside the Kenrokuen Exit stands the **Tsuzumi-mon** (鼓門), a 13.7-metre gate on two massive pillars. Its shape is inspired by the *tsuzumi*, the hand drum used in Kanazawa's traditional Noh theatre. Behind it is the glass **Motenashi Dome**. *Motenashi* means hospitality, and the idea is an umbrella held out to arriving guests in a city known for rain and snow.

The gate is lit from sunset until midnight. Every hour, on the hour, it glows for two minutes in one of the five traditional Kaga colours: a different colour each weekday, and all five in turn on weekends and holidays.

| Japanese | Say it like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 兼六園口はどちらですか？<br>*Kenrokuen-guchi wa dochira desu ka?* | ken-roh-koo-en goo-chee wah doh-chee-rah dess kah | Which way is the Kenrokuen Exit? |
| 観光案内所はどこですか？<br>*Kankō annaijo wa doko desu ka?* | kahn-koh ahn-nigh-joh wah doh-koh dess kah | Where is the tourist information center? |
| 大きいロッカーはありますか？<br>*Ōkii rokkā wa arimasu ka?* | oh-kee rock-kah wah ah-ree-mass kah | Are there any large lockers? |

*-guchi* (口) means exit or entrance, so *Kanazawa-kō-guchi* is the Kanazawa Port Exit. For more everyday phrases, see my [Japanese phrases for restaurants, shops and taxis](/articles/japanese-phrases-restaurants-shops-taxis/).

## Arriving at Kanazawa Station: the quick version

1. Pick your exit: **Kenrokuen (East)** for sightseeing, **Kanazawa Port (West)** for Shirakawa-go and airport buses.
2. Drop your bags: a locker, the left-luggage counter, or same-day delivery.
3. Get cash and a 1-day bus pass if you need them.
4. Walk out through the wooden gate to the Loop Bus at platform 7.
