**Short answer:** In Kanazawa you can pay by card or phone almost everywhere a visitor goes: the big sights, the station, convenience stores and the Loop Bus. Two things catch people out. **Regular Hokutetsu city buses don't accept Suica, ICOCA or PASMO** (use a contactless credit card instead), and a few places, like the **Ninja Temple** and many small stalls and bars, are **cash only**. Carry a card that works for contactless payment, plus some yen.

Japan is less cash-heavy than it used to be, but the rules still change from one counter to the next. Here is what the official pages say for the places most visitors go in Kanazawa, so you know what to have in your pocket.

*Information checked on Hokuriku Railway's (Hokutetsu's) Japanese pages, JR West's and PayPay's English pages, and the official pages of each sight, in November 2026. Payment options change, so check the official site if you are relying on one.*

## Do I need cash in Kanazawa?

Yes, some. Most of your day can be cashless, but not all of it:

- **Cash only:** the [Ninja Temple (Myoryuji)](/articles/ninja-temple-myoryuji-guide/) takes no cards and no electronic money.
- **Probably cash:** at [Omicho Market](/articles/omicho-market-guide/) each shop decides for itself, and I couldn't find market-wide card information. Small, older [izakaya](/articles/izakaya-english-kanazawa/) and bars can also be cash only.
- **Card is fine:** [Kenrokuen](/articles/kenrokuen-garden-guide/) lists credit cards, transit IC cards and e-money such as PayPay. [Kanazawa Castle Park](/articles/kanazawa-castle-park-guide/) lists cash, cards and e-money for its paid buildings.

A practical amount: enough yen for a morning at the market, a temple ticket and a couple of bus rides in case a card reader fails. Coins are useful too. On the Loop Bus, the fare box gives change for ¥1,000 notes and coins, **not for larger notes**.

## Where can I get cash with an overseas card?

At Kanazawa Station, the tourism association points visitors to the **ATM in the 7-Eleven** facing the concourse and the **Japan Post Bank ATM** inside the Anto shopping area. These are the ATMs it recommends for overseas cards. There is also a Travelex exchange counter in the station. My [Kanazawa Station guide](/articles/kanazawa-station-guide/) shows where they are.

7-Eleven ATMs are all over the city, so you rarely have to go far. Your own bank may charge a fee, so check that before you go.

## How do I pay on the bus?

This is the part most first-time visitors get wrong, so here is how it works, from Hokutetsu's own Japanese pages.

| Bus | Contactless credit card | Suica / ICOCA / PASMO | Cash |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kanazawa Loop Bus** (城下まち金沢周遊バス) | Yes | **Yes** | Yes |
| **Regular Hokutetsu city buses** (路線バス) | Yes | **No** | Yes |
| **Komatsu Airport Limousine Bus** | Yes | At the ticket machines | Yes |

**Contactless credit cards** ("touch payment", *tatchi kessai*) work on the Loop Bus, the regular city buses in the Kanazawa area and the Komatsu Airport bus. Hokutetsu lists **Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express, Diners Club, Discover and UnionPay**. A card in your phone's wallet works too, and there is no registration. Tap the **orange reader** when you get on, and tap the orange reader by the driver when you get off. You are charged the next day.

**Suica, ICOCA and other nationwide IC cards** work on the Loop Bus, but Hokutetsu says plainly that they **can't be used on regular route buses**. One more catch: if the Loop Bus runs with a regular route-bus vehicle that day, IC cards don't work on that bus either. That is why, for buses in Kanazawa, a contactless credit card is the most reliable thing to carry.

Hokutetsu also has its own card, **ICa**, which works on its buses and trains. For a short trip you don't need it. My [bus guide](/articles/kanazawa-bus-guide/) covers the routes, the 1-day pass and the back-door boarding on city buses.

## Should I get a Suica or ICOCA before I come?

It helps, but it isn't essential in Kanazawa.

- **ICOCA** is JR West's card. JR West sells a visitor design at **Kansai Airport Station** if you show a passport issued outside Japan, and on an iPhone you can add ICOCA to Apple Wallet and top it up with your own card.
- **Welcome Suica** is JR East's card for short-term visitors, mostly sold in the Tokyo area. JR East says it is valid for **28 days, including the day you buy it**.

In Kanazawa an IC card is handy for the Loop Bus, station coin lockers, convenience stores and the ticket windows at Kenrokuen. It won't help on the regular city buses, so don't plan your whole trip around it.

## Can I use PayPay or my own payment app?

Many small shops in Japan show a **PayPay** QR code at the till. PayPay is a Japanese app, but you may not need it. According to PayPay's June 2026 announcement, **35 overseas payment apps from 17 countries and regions** can now pay at PayPay shops in Japan. These include apps linked through Alipay+. If you use one of these apps at home, check its own information to see whether it works in Japan.

If you see a sign with many logos, just show the staff your app and ask (phrases below).

## Do I tip in Kanazawa?

No. Tipping isn't part of the culture in Japan, and leaving money on the table may just mean someone runs after you to give it back. Good service is included in the price.

What you may see instead is an extra charge on the bill:

- **Otōshi** (お通し): a small starter that some izakaya bring automatically, with a charge per person. My [izakaya guide](/articles/izakaya-english-kanazawa/) explains it.
- **Seat or table charge** at some bars.
- **Lodging tax**: Kanazawa adds an accommodation tax to hotel stays. My [where to stay guide](/articles/where-to-stay-kanazawa/) has the details.

## What about tax-free shopping?

Tax-free shopping changed on **November 1, 2026**. You now pay the full price, including tax, and get the tax back after customs checks your purchases when you leave Japan. I explained how it works in my [Japanese phrases for shops and taxis](/articles/japanese-phrases-restaurants-shops-taxis/).

## Say it in Japanese

| Japanese | Say it like | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| カードは使えますか？<br>*Kaado wa tsukaemasu ka?* | kah-doh wah tsoo-kah-eh-mass kah | Can I use a card? |
| 現金だけですか？<br>*Genkin dake desu ka?* | gen-keen dah-keh dess kah | Is it cash only? |
| タッチ決済できますか？<br>*Tatchi kessai dekimasu ka?* | tah-chee kes-sigh deh-kee-mass kah | Can I pay by tapping my card? |
| 領収書をお願いします。<br>*Ryōshūsho o onegaishimasu.* | ryoh-shoo-shoh oh oh-neh-gai-shee-mass | A receipt, please. |

*Genkin dake desu ka?* is the most useful one. Ask it before you order at a small counter, not after you've eaten. If the answer is *hai* (yes), you'll be glad you carried some yen.

## Paying checklist

1. **Bring a contactless credit card**, and check with your bank that it will work in Japan.
2. **Carry some cash**, including coins and ¥1,000 notes, for small shops, market stalls and the Ninja Temple.
3. **Buses:** a contactless card works on all Hokutetsu buses in town. Suica and ICOCA work on the Loop Bus only.
4. **ATMs:** use a 7-Eleven or a Japan Post Bank ATM.
5. **No tipping.** Expect an *otōshi* charge at some izakaya.
