**Short answer:** Yes, you can do it in a day. Take the Hokuriku Shinkansen from Kanazawa to **Kaga-Onsen Station** (the train I checked took **19 minutes**), then ride the **CANBUS** loop bus to the hot-spring towns. Each town has a public bath, called a *sōyu*, where a bath costs **¥500** for an adult. You don't need to stay at a ryokan to soak.

Kaga Onsen is the name for a group of hot-spring towns in Kaga City, south of Kanazawa. The three you can reach most easily are **Yamanaka**, **Yamashiro** and **Katayamazu**. Each feels different, and you probably have time for one or two in a day, not all three.

*Information checked on the official tourist association websites of Yamanaka, Yamashiro and Katayamazu, the official CANBUS site, and the JR West timetable on November 28, 2026. All of these pages are in Japanese. Hours and prices change, so check them again before you go.*

## How do I get from Kanazawa to Kaga Onsen?

By train to **Kaga-Onsen Station** (加賀温泉駅, *Kaga-onsen-eki*), then by bus. The hot-spring towns are not next to the station.

- **Shinkansen:** the Hokuriku Shinkansen stops at Kaga-Onsen, including some **Tsurugi** (つるぎ) trains, which run between Kanazawa and Tsuruga or Toyama. On the JR West timetable, one Tsurugi from Kaga-Onsen to Kanazawa took 19 minutes, with one stop at Komatsu. Not every train stops at Kaga-Onsen, so pick a train in a route app.
- **Local train:** the **IR Ishikawa Railway** (IRいしかわ鉄道) also runs between Kanazawa and Kaga-Onsen. It is a different company from JR, it is slower, and it is cheaper. If you have a rail pass, check whether your pass covers it before you board. I could not find that on the official pages.

If you are new to the Shinkansen, my [guide to getting to Kanazawa](/articles/getting-to-kanazawa/) explains the trains and reserved seats. For finding the platforms and lockers, see the [Kanazawa Station guide](/articles/kanazawa-station-guide/).

## How does the CANBUS work?

**CANBUS** (キャン・バス, *kyan-basu*) is the sightseeing loop bus of the Kaga area. It leaves from in front of **JR Kaga-Onsen Station** and runs three routes:

- **Yama-mawari** (山まわり線, "mountain loop"): goes toward Yamanaka and Yamashiro. The official site says a full loop takes about 1 hour 3 minutes to 1 hour 31 minutes.
- **Umi-mawari** (海まわり線, "sea loop"): goes toward Katayamazu and the coast. About 1 hour 23 minutes for a loop.
- **Komatsu Airport line** (小松空港線): about 30 to 40 minutes one way.

Fares on the official site:

| Ticket | Adult price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| One ride | ¥500 | Pay into the fare box when you get on. Elementary school children pay half. |
| One-day digital ticket | ¥1,300 | **Not sold on the bus.** Buy it in an app before you ride. |

The official site lists several apps that sell the one-day ticket, including **tabiwa by WESTER** (JR West's travel app) and Jorudan's mobile ticket. If you plan to visit two towns, the one-day ticket is cheaper than paying for each ride. Look at the [official timetable](https://www.kaga-canbus.jp/) before you go, because the loops run at fixed times and you don't want to wait an hour at a stop.

## Which town should I choose?

### Yamanaka Onsen: the gorge

Yamanaka sits in a valley along the **Kakusenkei Gorge** (鶴仙渓, *Kakusen-kei*), with a walking path by the river and an old wooden bridge called **Korogi-bashi** (こおろぎ橋). This is the one to choose if you want a walk in nature as well as a bath.

The public bath is **Kiku-no-yu** (菊の湯). According to the Yamanaka Onsen tourist association:

- **Hours:** 7:00 to 22:00
- **Price:** adults ¥500
- **Closed:** the 2nd and 4th Tuesday of each month (plus occasional maintenance days)
- The men's bath and the women's bath are in **separate buildings**, which the association says is rare in Japan.

In the warmer months there is a riverside seating area on the gorge called **Kakusenkei Kawadoko** (鶴仙渓川床). The official page says it runs **from April to November**, so don't plan around it in winter.

### Yamashiro Onsen: the old bathhouse

Yamashiro is built around a small square with two public baths side by side. This is the town to choose if you want something that feels historic.

- **Sōyu** (総湯), the modern public bath: 6:00 to 22:00, adults **¥500**.
- **Kosōyu** (古総湯), a rebuilt Meiji-era bathhouse: adults **¥700**. Hours are 6:00 to 22:00, but **7:00 to 21:00 from December to February**.
- A combined ticket for both is **¥1,000**.
- Both are closed on the **4th Wednesday of each month from 6:00 to 12:00**, and open as usual from noon.

One important thing about Kosōyu: the official page says it has **no taps or showers, and you cannot use soap or shampoo**. It is a place to soak, not to wash. If you want to wash properly, use Sōyu next door first.

### Katayamazu Onsen: the lake

Katayamazu is on the shore of **Lake Shibayama** (柴山潟, *Shibayama-gata*). Its public bath, also called **Sōyu** (総湯), is a glass building by the lake, and the Katayamazu tourist association says you can see the lake from one of the baths.

- **Hours:** 6:00 to 22:00
- **Price:** adults **¥500**
- **Closed:** no regular closing day, but it closes once a month for maintenance and sometimes on other days

Katayamazu is on the sea loop of the CANBUS, while Yamanaka is on the mountain loop, so pairing those two in one day means using both routes. Check the timetable for where and when they connect.

## Is there anything else on the way?

**Natadera** (那谷寺), a temple set among rocks and trees, is one of the places the CANBUS serves (check which loop on the timetable). It is in Komatsu City, not Kaga. The Kaga tourism site (tabimati.net) lists the hours as **9:15 to 16:00** and admission at **¥1,000** for junior high school students and older. I could not open the temple's own website when I checked, so confirm the hours before you go.

## How does a public bath work?

If you have never been to a Japanese bath, the basic flow is the same everywhere:

1. Pay at the counter or ticket machine.
2. Go to the changing room for your gender. Leave your clothes in a basket or locker.
3. **Wash your body before you get in the bath.** (Kosōyu in Yamashiro has no showers, so wash at Sōyu next door first.)
4. No swimsuits. Keep your small towel out of the water.
5. Get out slowly. Hot water and a cold day are a dizzy combination, so drink some water.

Don't expect hotel-style amenities: the Yamashiro page, for example, says Sōyu does not put out toiletries. Bring a small towel, or ask at the counter what they sell. Rules on tattoos are not something I found on the official pages, so if that matters to you, ask before you pay.

## Say it in Japanese

> **Otona hitori desu.** (oh-toh-nah hee-toh-ree dess) — "One adult, please." Say "futari" (foo-tah-ree) for two.

> **Kono basu wa Yamanaka Onsen ni ikimasu ka?** (koh-noh bah-soo wah yah-mah-nah-kah ohn-sen nee ee-kee-mass kah) — "Does this bus go to Yamanaka Onsen?" Swap in Yamashiro or Katayamazu.

> **Taoru wa arimasu ka?** (tah-oh-roo wah ah-ree-mass kah) — "Do you have towels?"

> **Eki ni modoru basu wa nanji desu ka?** (eh-kee nee moh-doh-roo bah-soo wah nahn-jee dess kah) — "What time is the bus back to the station?"

## A simple day plan

1. Leave your big bags in Kanazawa, so you can hop on and off the bus with just a daypack.
2. Morning Shinkansen or local train to Kaga-Onsen Station. Buy the one-day CANBUS ticket in the app.
3. Mountain loop to **Yamanaka**: walk the gorge, then bathe at Kiku-no-yu.
4. Back on the loop to **Yamashiro** for the old bathhouse.
5. Bus back to Kaga-Onsen Station and train to Kanazawa for dinner.

In winter, add some time for snow. My [winter guide](/articles/kanazawa-winter-guide/) covers what to wear, and a hot bath is one of the best things to do on a cold, grey day. It also works as a plan B for bad weather, along with the ideas in my [rainy day guide](/articles/rainy-day-kanazawa/). If you are choosing between day trips, compare this with the [Shirakawa-go day trip](/articles/shirakawa-go-day-trip/), which takes most of a day by bus.
