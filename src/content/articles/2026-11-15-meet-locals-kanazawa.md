**Short answer:** the easiest way to meet locals in Kanazawa is to join an event where locals are already hoping to meet you. My English school runs a casual international exchange event about once a month. Visitors are welcome, it costs around ¥2,000, and nobody expects you to speak Japanese.

I'll be upfront: this is my own school's event. I'm writing about it because it is the honest answer to a question travelers keep searching for, and because I can tell you exactly how it works.

## Why it's hard to meet locals in Kanazawa

Kanazawa is friendly, but it is also a fairly quiet, polite city. People are helpful if you ask for directions. They are less likely to start a conversation with a stranger at the next table.

There is also a language gap. Many people here studied English at school and understand more than they can say. They would love to talk to you, but they are nervous about making mistakes.

So most visitors leave having spoken mainly to hotel staff, shop staff and other travelers. That is a shame, because the people who live here are a big part of what makes the city good.

If you search for "meet locals in Kanazawa", most of what you'll find are paid tours: a local guide takes you to bars or around the old districts. Those can be great. But there is another option.

## The exchange events at my school

I run [Regrandir English](https://regrandir.com/), a small English school in Nonoichi, the city right next to Kanazawa. Most of my students are beginners who want to use English, not just study it.

About once a month, the school holds an **international exchange event**. It is not a class. It is a relaxed get-together where local people and people from overseas spend time together and talk, in English and in Japanese, whichever works.

A few things to know:

- **You don't have to be a student.** The events are open to non-students.
- **The fee is around ¥2,000.** It covers the actual costs of the day, and it changes a little depending on what we do.
- **You don't need any Japanese.** Many of the local participants are learning English and are happy to use it.
- **It's casual.** It is people spending time together, not a lesson.

## What the events are like

Each event is different. Past ones have included:

- a barbecue
- bowling and games at an entertainment center
- an English "road trip"
- a cherry blossom picnic (*ohanami*) in spring
- a mölkky tournament (a Finnish lawn game that is popular in Japan)
- a New Year party

For a traveler, this is a rare chance to see an ordinary side of Ishikawa life: how people here spend a weekend afternoon, what they eat, what they want to know about your country.

## Say it in Japanese

If you do come, these will make you a lot of friends very quickly:

> **Hajimemashite.** (hah-jee-meh-mash-teh) — "Nice to meet you."
> **[Your country] kara kimashita.** (kah-rah kee-mash-tah) — "I'm from [your country]."
> **Kanazawa no osusume wa nan desu ka?** (kah-nah-zah-wah no oh-soo-soo-meh wah nahn dess kah) — "What do you recommend in Kanazawa?"

That last question works especially well. Locals love being asked, and you will get better answers than any guidebook. For more phrases, see my [guide to ordering at an izakaya](/articles/izakaya-english-kanazawa/).

## Not here on the right date?

The events happen about once a month, so if your dates don't match, here are some other ways to connect with local life:

- Sit at the counter, not at a table, in small restaurants and bars. Conversations start more easily there. My [guide to Kanazawa's nightlife districts](/articles/kanazawa-nightlife-areas/) explains where to go.
- Visit Omicho Market in the morning and ask the stall staff what is good today.
- Send the school a message anyway and ask when the next event is.
